## scan-1: which machine for each stage (the Town Brain, 9 min)

- **stage1_map_machine**: DELL
- **why**: Linux natively supports Python 3.12/PyTorch; 32 GB RAM safely absorbs MapAnything's CPU fallback; avoids MILL's outdated Python 3.9 and MINI's constrained 20 GB shared memory.
- **stage2_photoreal_machine**: MILL
- **why2**: RX 560 GPU and 882 GB disk are required for Brush's photo-real reconstruction and large .ply output; Dell lacks GPU acceleration and Mini's 11 GB usable disk will freeze during build/output.
- **biggest_risk**: MILL's macOS 10.14 environment may lack modern compiler support or GPU drivers, causing Brush to fail or freeze despite hardware specs.
- **the_check_that_proves_it**: Validate DELL runs Python 3.12 and completes a MapAnything CPU fallback test under 6 hours; confirm MILL's OS can install Brush's dependencies or run a compatible Linux binary before committing.
- **what_you_would_fetch_yourself_first**: The 4.91 GB MapAnything checkpoint, Rust 1.88 toolchain for MINI fallback, and Chrome 134+ for the browser viewer, ensuring MILL disk is pre-cleared.

**CAIRN's note (a disagreement, not checked on the machine yet):** the Brain's own biggest risk is real. Brush needs a modern graphics layer, and the Mill runs macOS 10.14 from 2018, so stage 2 on the Mill is likely to fail. The strongest graphics card in the house is the Radeon VII (16 GB) on the Mini, but there's no ready-made Brush for Intel Macs, so it would mean building from source, and the Mini's disk is under its 30 GB floor. Stage 1 on the Dell (CPU) is agreed. Stage 2 is OPEN until a 10-minute test on a sample room proves a machine can do it.
