**P1. What each stage changes (visible on screen)**
Each power stage visibly reshapes the colony’s daily routines through AR overlays. **Muscle** (cotton-reel treadmill) lets dust-bunnies haul heavier pebbles and stack cardboard bricks into taller scaffolds; on screen, you’ll see reinforced ropes, faster foot-paddling animations, and newly accessible high shelves. **Water** (bottle-cap wheel on straw pipes) introduces buoyancy and flow: floating leaf-rafts carry supplies across gaps, while visible condensation drips carve new slip-n-slide routes. **Wind** (windowsill pinwheel) manifests as swirling dust-mote currents that lift tissue-paper flags, reveal hidden crevices under books, and carry lightweight seeds to new foraging zones. **Steam** (thimble kettle + train) transforms thermal zones: rising vapor clouds push up temporary platforms, melt frost-cameras into clear lenses, and power chuffing elevator shafts that extend upward. **Electricity** (potato battery + solar cell) activates glowing circuit-trails on screen, illuminating dark corners, making static-charged dust-bunnies leap between objects, and triggering sensor-flaps that open sealed storage nooks. Each stage doesn’t just add mechanics—it visually rewires the colony’s architecture, transport, and resource distribution, making the invisible forces of physics suddenly tangible and playable.

**P2. One funny mishap per stage**
Each stage births a harmless, slapstick mishap that resets playfully. **Muscle:** The cotton-reel treadmill spins too fast, flinging a dust-bunny into a yarn ball. They emerge tangled but giggling, using the yarn as a lasso to pull down a dangling snack. **Water:** The bottle-cap wheel jams, reversing flow and spraying a miniature fountain. A dust-bunny dons a thimble as a rainhat, attempts to sail a paper boat, then uses a matchstick to bail out comically. **Wind:** The pinwheel catches a stray eraser, spinning violently and launching a feather pillow. Three dust-bunnies get wrapped in it, then discover it’s a perfect parachute for gliding from the bookshelf. **Steam:** The thimble kettle whistles too loudly, shooting a steam jet that inflates a balloon. When it pops, it showers paper confetti; the creatures wear the scraps as party hats, dancing to the kettle’s off-key tune. **Electricity:** The potato battery overcharges, sending static arcs that make the dust-bunnies’ fur bristle like dandelions. They bounce off each other like pogo sticks until a metal spoon grounds them, leaving them in a heap of laughing static-zaps.

**P3. The weakest part & alternative**
The weakest element is the “power ladder” framework itself. It implies a rigid hierarchy where electricity is the “peak,” which clashes with the game’s domestic, nature-adjacent tone and AR’s potential for non-linear exploration. Children aged 7+ thrive on cyclical, cause-and-effect play, not vertical progression. Forcing a ladder risks making earlier stages feel like disposable tutorials rather than living systems. I’d replace it with a **Resonance Web**: stages aren’t ranked but interwoven. Instead of climbing, players rotate household items to create feedback loops—steam meets wind to spin a paper turbine, water meets muscle to power a wooden mill, static meets solar cells to charge a glow-lantern. The AR would show these interactions as glowing threads connecting real-world surfaces, emphasizing circulation over conquest. This better matches the 90s cartoon’s gentle ethos, encourages experimental play, and lets the colony evolve organically rather than “level up.” The ladder’s competitive framing is unnecessary; a web of domestic physics feels more magical, more AR-native, and more true to the dust-bunnies’ world.

### Stage 3: Brain (words only), untouched

**1. Style B reads best.** Shiny plastic and chunky shapes deliver high contrast and bold silhouettes, essential for clarity on an iPad screen amidst real-world lounge clutter. This style creates a distinct "game layer" that pops against reality without visual competition. I disagree with A and C: A's "grubby" textures risk appearing muddy or low-contrast on a tablet, and wobbly ink may vibrate against real-world edges, causing visual fatigue. C's fine details (visible cut edges) will likely blur at arm's length, and paper textures often clash with real-room lighting, breaking AR immersion. B also thematically wins; it frames household junk as toys, enhancing the "toy war" fantasy.

**2. Rule: "Material Resistances."** Tie the fort's construction to its defense. Matchbox towers absorb rockets but shatter against swarms; cushion walls squash rockets but bounce swarms; bottle-cap turrets deflect rockets but jam swarms. This prevents a meta because the optimal weapon depends entirely on the opponent's random pile of odds and ends. Players must scout the fort and adapt, ensuring the build phase drives the strategy. No weapon is king; the pile of junk decides the winner.
