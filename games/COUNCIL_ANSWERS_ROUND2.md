R4. Funny: You set down a coffee mug; they treat it as a lagoon, rafting on a coaster. You play bass-heavy music; they form a tiny mosh pit that bounces the huts like drums. You crack the door open; a draft hits, and they panic-dance while weaving a straw windbreak. Touching: You leave for hours; they dim their berry-lights, huddle together, and leave a single polished pebble at the edge of the frame as a "we kept your spot warm" marker.

R5. I disagree with the "up to a day" persistence limit. A living world should remember weeks, not just hours. If they reset too often, they feel like a screensaver, not a colony. The hook is waking up to a changed world that breathes with your room’s rhythm. No quests, no timers. Just the quiet satisfaction of seeing them grow, swap neighbors, and react to your real habits.

R6. Scan a friend’s room to send "dust parcels" (seeds, tools, or jokes) that appear as gifts. Trade rare berries via QR codes. Host "tower contests" where each colony tries to build highest on their own surfaces. Write letters by drawing on paper; the other colony "reads" them by scanning the page.

R7. The Hoover approaches; they scramble into a book gap, rebuild the doorframe, and leave a "we survived" flag. A cat looms; they use its tail as a mountain to climb, then scatter playfully before it pounces. A cup spills; they quickly stack coasters into a dam, then float on leaf-boats to safety. All handled with wobbly, resilient cartoon logic. The key is forgiveness: if you squash a hut, they just rebuild elsewhere with a shrug, not a penalty.

R8. They only truly evolve when you’re not looking. Staring too long makes them pause to "rest" or "perform." You must trust them to live while you step away.
