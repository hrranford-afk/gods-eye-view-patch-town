# Baguettes & Ballistics: design

A toy war across a real kitchen. Tiny vehicles, real-world scale, everyday
things as terrain. Fun first on an iPad: one thumb drives, one thumb fights
and builds.

## The loop

1. **Gather.** Diggers dig loot from piles; dump trucks haul it to your toy
   box (the factory) or to bottle caps you hold (depots).
2. **Build.** Spend loot on turrets, vehicles, workers and bridges. Each
   thing has a recipe once there are several kinds of loot.
3. **Fight.** Hold caps for shorter hauls, raid enemy trucks (wrecks spill
   their load), defend your own routes.
4. **Win.** Break the enemy toy box.

The enemy plays by the same rules: it digs, hauls, builds and upgrades.

## The twist: race, then battle (Harry's idea, 31 Oct 2026)

**Status: built for level 1 (2 Nov 2026).** A 2-lap race with 8 gates, 3 red rivals and 8 supply boxes; dropped and carried boxes unfold into turrets, sugar walls and workers; ramming steals; the winner gets +30 cereal and the last place a free gun turret; Skip race jumps to the battle. Details in HANDOVER.md, "Level 1 race opening". Levels 2 to 6 now open with the Loadout Race (a short circuit and tool tokens, not box-carrying); level 1 keeps this race and adds the tokens. Not yet built: ambush spots as the catch-up gift, a 3-lap option, other levels' tracks.

Each level starts as a race and turns into a war.

1. **Race.** Laps around the table. Supply boxes lie on the track.
   Pick one up, carry it, and drop it anywhere you like: at home, or
   at a spot you want to hold. The box shows what it becomes (gun,
   wall, truck, digger), so every pickup is a choice. Carrying slows
   you a little: grab more, or race faster?
2. **The switch.** The kettle clicks, the table shakes, and every
   dropped box unfolds into what it holds. Your base is wherever you
   raced it to, so every game has a different map.
3. **Battle.** As now: cereal keeps the war going, and you break the
   enemy toy box to win.

- **Steal (CAIRN's add):** ram a car carrying a box and it drops it.
  The race has fights in it, and grudges carry into the battle.
- **Keep it close:** the last-place racer gets a catch-up gift (a free
  turret or an ambush spot), so losing the race never loses the game.
- **Why it works:** the race teaches driving before building, which
  fixes the hard first minutes.

### The finale, level 6 (Harry's design, built as stage 1 on 2 Nov 2026)

- **The map:** a worktop in a U, about 1.46x the normal table. The middle of the U is a solid block (the cut-out) with the kettle on it. Your toy box is at the tip of the left arm (the sink: water, ferries, boats), theirs at the tip of the right arm (the breadboard). The bottom bend is the battlefield and is the hob (hot rings, the cook's pan) with the paperclips. Warm night light: dim, but readable. The camera pulls back.
- **Phase 1, the build-up (60 s).** The kettle boils (countdown, rising kettle sounds). A glowing ceasefire line across the bend: nobody crosses it and nobody shoots over it. Both sides gather, build turrets and train squadrons; the enemy builds where you can see it (hold Peek).
- **Phase 2, WAR.** The kettle clicks, a banner says WAR! and shows both battle plans, the line drops, and the normal battle runs (the clock restarts at 0, the enemy at full aggression).
- **Squadrons** (one thumb, one order each): **Baguette bombers** (3 flying baguettes: over water and walls, crumb bombs with splash, strong against turrets, fragile; rocket turrets counter them), **Boat flotilla** (2 gunboats in the sink), **Tank column** (3), **Raider pack** (4, hunt trucks). Orders: **Attack** (their fortress, by your chosen lane), **Defend** (your rally marker), **Raid** (their trucks). 24 units a side at most. The enemy builds squadrons too.
- **The battle plan (CAIRN's add):** in the last 10 s of the build-up pick Left, Middle or Right. The enemy picks in secret (its attackers wait at the gate it probably uses: a tell). Attack squadrons go by the lane; a defence that covers the enemy's lane takes 25% less damage there. Reward for reading the enemy, nothing more.
- **Stage 2 (BUILT, 2 Nov 2026):**
  - **Infantry** (Harry's idea: everything you collected all game becomes your army): Cereal Loopers (6, swarm, grab caps), Paperclip Pikemen (4, armoured, extra damage to cars, hold a line on Defend), Pea Shooters (4, float over the sink, lob over walls), Battery Bots (2, a pulse every few seconds stuns turrets and cars for 1.5 s). They cost peas and batteries (new stocks on this level, from small risky piles) and count half a unit toward the cap. The enemy builds them too.
  - **THE BAGUETTE** (3:00 of war): an armoured bread train on a fixed track round the U (faint rails on the worktop), a crusty engine and four carriages, each its own target with its own weapon borrowed from an earlier boss (Stapler: snaps shut turrets; Duck: bubble lobs; Remote: snipes; Toaster: lobs flaming toast). Destroyed carriages fall off; the engine can only be hurt once they are all gone; its death pops the shield on their toy box and spills cereal.
  - **The Great Domino Run:** the longest run in the game, from a button at the hob bend up the enemy arm to their fortress: 500 damage and it breaks the Baguette's shield (a comeback move). Needs the button weight (infantry count half). Resets after 90 s. Red has a smaller run (250) aimed at you.
  - **The fridge door** (3:00 of war): the door opens, light floods the table for 20 s (the existing lights, brightened), then it slams and the scene is darker for good, still readable.
  - **The ending:** destroying their fortress flicks the kitchen light on, then MEALTIME MAYHEM COMPLETE shows your stars for all six levels out of 18, with Play again and Levels.
- **Walls:** a Sugar wall button builds a solid block across your path (12 cereal, 6 at most); the enemy builds a few at its gate.

**Between levels:** a short "day in the kitchen" card (morning, then lunch,
then dinner) and a garage where stars unlock paint jobs and horns. Unlocks are
for fun, never for power, so a new player can still win.

**Race-then-battle** (above) is the opening of every level from level 2
onwards, once the race code exists. The track runs around that level's hazards,
so the race teaches the new rule before the fight.

## The Gumball Machine (Harry's idea, 1 Nov 2026)

A big gumball machine in the middle of a level. Ram its buttons and it
rattles, spins and drops a gumball. 10 cereal a spin. The colour is the prize:

- **Red, base:** instant turret level-up, or a shield on your toy box.
- **Blue, car:** turbo, double damage or armour for 30 seconds.
- **Yellow, map:** a cereal airdrop, a free truck, or a bridge.
- **Black, dud:** a whoopee cushion.

**CAIRN's add:** it sits where the fighting is, and both teams can use it,
so guarding the machine becomes its own battle.

**Fixed rule:** spins only ever cost game cereal. No real money, no paid
spins, no loot boxes, ever.

## The Kitchen Grand Prix (Harry's idea, 1 Nov 2026)

**Status: built, in a smaller form, as the Loadout Race (2 Nov 2026).** Before every level you race 3 red cars round a small circuit on that level's table; tokens round the track are the tool buttons, you carry 4 at most, and finishing position sets starting cereal (1st also a free Upgrade) and the enemy's own limited loadout. Only the tools you win get buttons in the battle. Not built from the original idea below: laps with item boxes, the campaign map branching, the carried-item bonus. Details in HANDOVER.md, "Loadout Race".

A short battle race *between* levels, a different kind of game from the
battles:

- 2–3 laps on a little kitchen track (round the fruit bowl, over a chopping
  board ramp, under the toast rack), with gumball item boxes on the track.
- **It decides where you go next:** the campaign map branches; 1st picks the
  next level, otherwise the enemy picks the one that suits them.
- **And how you start:** 1st gets a free Monster truck and an extra dump
  truck, 2nd gets 30 cereal, last still gets a catch-up turret.
- **CAIRN's add:** an item still in your hand at the finish line comes with
  you into the battle.
- The race-at-the-start-of-a-level (dropped boxes become your base) stays as
  the opening of the battle itself.

## Kitchen hazards and set pieces

- **Sink:** water gap; build a bridge (spaghetti: cheap, snaps · lolly
  sticks: sturdy · ruler: heavy, needs a crane). Hover skims across. *Built in level 4 with one fixed ruler bridge, ferries and boats; the crane is still to do.*
- **Hob:** rings heat and cool on a cycle; hot rings hurt. *Built in level 5.*
- **Oven:** door drops as a ramp to a high ledge with long sight lines. *Built in level 5.*
- **Toaster:** launcher over walls.
- **Kettle:** boils on a timer; steam blocks sight.
- **Fruit bowl:** central fortress.
- **Fridge:** cold zone slows everything; batteries inside.
- **Microwave:** turntable spins whatever sits on it.
- **Zip line:** dental floss from the spice rack; trucks ride it.
- **Lift:** wind a cupboard handle to reach the microwave top.

## How it should feel

Morning sun through the window, long shadows; shiny plastic toys on real
materials (wood, steel, tile); crumbs, steam, drips; clicky toy sounds; the
kettle click as a countdown. Night levels lit by the fridge.

## Engine notes

- three.js r128, one HTML file per build, no assets (everything is built
  from shapes), keeps it light on an iPad.
- Route finding: A* on a 2-unit grid, rebuilt when blocks change; units
  steer straight when the way is clear.
- Next engine work: heights (worktop, hob, oven ledge), bridges as
  buildable walkways, a minimap and pull-back camera for the big U.
