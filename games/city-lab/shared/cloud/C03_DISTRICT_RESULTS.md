## Formulas (all coefficients live in the district file; details in DISTRICT.md)

```
residents(home)        = capacity (data); workers per home = round(residents * work_fraction)
reach(home, shop)      = open road steps between their access cells + 1; none if blocked or > max_reach_cells
weight w(h,s)          = decay_per_cell ^ reach
spend(h)               = ((residents - employed) + employed * (1 + wage_spend_boost)) * per_resident_per_min[item]
demand_from_h(s)       = spend(h) * w(h,s) / max(1, sum of w(h,s') over shops of the same item)
footfall(s)            = min(cap, market_bonus * sum over markets of footfall.decay ^ reach(s,m))
market visitors(s)     = sum over markets of visitors[item] * footfall.decay ^ reach(m,s) / max(1, sum over shops)
demand(s)              = sum_h demand_from_h(s) * (1 + footfall(s)) + visitors(s)      -> sim demand_per_minute
grid draw              = sum of recipe demand_kw + draw_kw + home_base_kw + per_resident_kw * residents
brownout factor        = min(1, supply_kw * scale / draw)  -> every machine gets supply_kw = factor * its demand_kw
deliveries             = carts run only if an open road path joins the depot to the building
staffing               = places filled by reachable workers / places (hire_priority order, nearest home first)
```

- Demand rises with every home added: 8.1, 12.9, 16.9, 20.6, 25.3 loaves a minute at 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 homes.
- It rises more slowly than the number of residents: demand per resident falls from 1.01 to 0.64 (2 to 8 homes), because each new home stands further from the bakery and distance decay cuts what it buys.
- Workers are the first limit, but not for the bakery: with 2 homes only 4 of 5 job places can be filled (half of each home works). The bakery is hired first and is fully staffed (3/3); the workshop runs on 1/2. From 4 homes every place is filled and more homes add customers, not staff.
- Power is the next limit: at 8 homes the grid draw is 13.7 kW against a 13.0 kW supply, so every building runs at 95% power and the bakery bakes slower.
- The bakery itself is the last limit: at 10 homes demand (25.3) is above what it can sell (23.1) and 2.2 customers a minute leave empty-handed (its capacity is 25.7 a minute and power already slows it).
- Income per minute at the bakery: 32.4, 51.6, 67.6, 82.4, 92.4; net per minute: 5.6, 20.6, 32.5, 44.8, 55.3.

## EXP3: two identical bakeries in two neighbourhoods

Both run the same `recipes/bakery.json`. A: 4 homes, bakery at 5,3 next to the market at 6,1, supply 12 kW. B: 2 homes, bakery at the far end 7,3, market at 0,1, supply 7.4 kW, the road at 5,2 blocked from 150 s to 330 s. Both get a 20% grid dip from 200 s to 400 s. Control: both in condition A.

|  | A | B | control 1 | control 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| homes reaching the bakery | 4 | 2 | 4 | 4 |
| demand/min | 12.6 | 3.2 | 12.6 | 12.6 |
| sold/min | 12.0 | 2.9 | 12.0 | 12.0 |
| lost customers/min | 0.5 | 0.2 | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| made/min | 16.8 | 8.4 | 16.8 | 16.8 |
| spoiled | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| cart visits missed | 0 | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| brownout time (share of run) | 0.00 | 0.33 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| income/min | 48.0 | 11.6 | 48.0 | 48.0 |
| net/min | 10.0 | -15.7 | 10.0 | 10.0 |
| state hash | 5ea921ea | 256d60c6 | 5ea921ea | 5ea921ea |

Cause by cause (start from B, change one thing to A's value):

| change | net/min | change in net/min | demand/min | sold/min | cart visits missed | brownout share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| homes | -7.57 | 8.11 | 5.4 | 5.1 | 2 | 1.00 |
| location | -7.19 | 8.49 | 5.5 | 5.2 | 2 | 0.33 |
| road | -17.18 | -1.50 | 4.6 | 4.3 | 0 | 0.33 |
| power | -15.67 | 0.00 | 3.2 | 2.9 | 2 | 0.00 |
| all four (should equal A) | 9.99 | 25.67 | 12.6 | 12.0 | 0 | 0.00 |

- Control: two bakeries in condition A give the same numbers to the last digit (net 9.99 each), so every difference below comes from the neighbourhood, not from luck or the recipe.
- Result: bakery A nets 10.0 a minute, bakery B -15.7. Demand 12.6 against 3.2; sold 12.0 against 2.9; missed cart visits 0 against 2; brownout time 0% against 33%; spoiled 0 and 0.
- Cause 1, homes: A has 4 homes (16 residents), B has 2 (8). Giving B the 4 homes alone moves its net by 8.1 a minute (demand 5.4).
- Cause 2, location: A stands next to the market and the homes, B at the far end of the street. Moving only the bakery and the market to A's places moves B's net by 8.5 (demand 5.5): shorter road distance, more footfall and market visitors.
- Cause 3, the blocked road: for 180 s B is cut off, its customers cannot come (demand is 0 then) and 2 cart visits are missed. Opening it alone moves B's net by -1.5 (demand 4.6 against 3.2). The missed carts did not starve the oven: B still made 8.4 loaves a minute against 2.9 sold, so the shelves held enough flour for the gap. The cost of the road is lost customers.
- Cause 4, the power margin: both are hit by the same 20% grid dip; A has a 4.4 kW margin and does not feel it, B browns out for 33% of the run. Giving B A's margin alone changes its net by 0.00: B sells so little that slower baking still keeps up. Power matters only to a bakery that is close to its capacity.
- The four changes are not additive (they interact): homes +8.1, location +8.5, road -1.5, power +0.0; all four together +25.7 and B becomes A exactly.

## Honest limits

- One district file, one set of coefficients. They are plausible toy numbers, not measured ones; the conclusions are about this data. Change `districts/patch_row.json` and run again.
- Residents move in at once and never leave; no births, no mood, no individual walking: only counts and road distance.
- The cart delivery has no travel time; only "is there an open road" matters.
- Windows are 10 simulated minutes. The first minutes of EXP3 include the start-up transient (stock filling); EXP1 and EXP2 skip the first 5 minutes.
- The cool store room cost includes a one-off while the bigger flour shelf fills; the wage rise is permanent.
- Software run time of the whole experiment set: 14.9 s for 3 repeats.

## Erratum (C03b, 21 Nov)
The windows above include the one-off cost of filling the shelves (ingredients are charged on delivery), so absolute net figures are low by about 5 to 7 a minute (experiments 1 and 2) and about 16 (experiment 3). At steady state the three conclusions hold in sign and ranking; bakery B is break-even (-0.1), not -15.7; the store room loses about 3.0, not 6 to 11; the road cause is -0.2 (B) and -4.7 (A), not -1.5. Full explanation, numbers and tests: shared/cloud/C03b_ODDITIES.md.
