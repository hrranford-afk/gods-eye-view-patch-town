- Shipped network, 10 homes: the generators give 13.5 kW and the cables ask for 13.5 kW, so the source is NOT short. One more 3 kW generator costs 66.67 and 0.60 a minute; district net per minute 87.66 to 87.66 (-0.60 after upkeep). Answer: NO, it never pays back.
- Shipped network, 4 homes: the generators give 13.5 kW and the cables ask for 12.1 kW, so the source is NOT short. One more 3 kW generator costs 66.67 and 0.60 a minute; district net per minute 39.50 to 39.50 (-0.60 after upkeep). Answer: NO, it never pays back.
- Shipped network, 8 homes: the generators give 13.5 kW and the cables ask for 13.5 kW, so the source is NOT short. One more 3 kW generator costs 66.67 and 0.60 a minute; district net per minute 69.38 to 69.38 (-0.60 after upkeep). Answer: NO, it never pays back.
- Sensitivity (a smaller first generator, 12.0 kW), 10 homes: the lowest power share is 67% before and 75% after; net per minute 79.45 to 87.66, +7.61 after the 0.60 upkeep. Pays back in 8.8 minutes.
- Sensitivity (a smaller first generator, 9.0 kW), 10 homes: the lowest power share is 50% before and 67% after; net per minute 57.48 to 79.45, +21.38 after the 0.60 upkeep. Pays back in 3.1 minutes.

**Answer.** On the shipped network the answer is **NO** at 10 homes and at 4 homes, and also at 8: the 13.5 kW generator already covers what the cables can carry (the homes cable caps the homes street at 3.0 kW), so a second generator adds no power to anyone and only adds upkeep. This is partly BY CONSTRUCTION: the shipped generator was sized to equal the capped demand so that E5 can show a clean local dim. The sensitivity rows (a smaller first generator) show when it WOULD pay: only when the generator is the limit and the shops, not the homes, are short. The homes street never earns anything, so power that only reaches homes never pays in this version. Do not read the sensitivity rows as the shipped data.

## E5. An overloaded cable dims ONLY its own street (8 homes)

| case | homes get | main street gets | delivered kW | district net /min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as built (homes cable 3.0 kW) | 94% | 100% | 13.50 | 69.38 |
| homes cable upgraded to 3.2 kW | 99% | 99% | 13.50 | 69.38 |
| main feeder cut to 8 kW (homes cable large enough) | 100% | 76% | 11.20 | 63.82 |

- As built, 8 homes: the homes cable wants 3.2 kW and carries 3.0 kW (red). Homes get 94% of their power; the main feeder wants 10.5 of 12.0 kW (green) and every building on it gets 100%. Only the homes street is dim.
- Upgrade the homes cable to 3.2 kW: it costs 2.67 and 0.02 a minute (linear rule, illustrative). Homes go to 99%, delivered power 13.50 to 13.50 kW, district net per minute 69.38 to 69.38 (0.00). The bottleneck then moves to the generator: it gives 13.5 kW and the cables now ask for 13.7 kW, so every branch is a little short. It never pays back in coins: homes have no machine, so a dim street changes no money in this version.
- The other street: with the main feeder cut to 8.0 kW and the homes cable large enough, the main street wants 10.5 kW, gets 76% and the homes stay at 100%. District net per minute 69.38 to 63.82 (-5.55): this is where a brownout touches money.
- The same upgrade at 10 homes (homes cable to 4.0 kW): the cables now ask for 14.5 kW but the generator gives 13.5 kW, so rule A takes the shortage from EVERY branch: homes 75% to 93%, the shops 100% to 93%; the bakery nets 67.89 to 63.62 and the district 87.66 to 83.55 (-4.11 a minute). A bigger homes cable LOSES money here: the homes earn nothing and the shops lose power.

**Answer.** Yes, it dims only its own street, exactly: with the homes cable overloaded every home gets 94% and every shop and the workshop get exactly 100%; with the main feeder overloaded the shops dim and the homes stay at 100%. Upgrading the homes cable costs 2.67 (plus 0.02 a minute) and earns **nothing** in coins (0.00 a minute), because homes have no machine in this version: a dim home street changes no money. The only overload that costs money is the one on the shops' cable (-5.55 a minute). After the upgrade the generator becomes the limit (it gives 13.5 kW, the cables now ask for 13.7). At 10 homes the same upgrade is worse than useless: it makes the generator short and rule A takes power from the shops, so the district net FALLS by 4.11 a minute (the owner chose rule A; a priority rule B, homes last, would not do this).

## What a DARK street costs (8 homes, steady state; also three identical runs: true)

| case | district net /min | bakery net /min | bakery sold /min | bakery costs /min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as built | 69.38 | 52.57 | 20.54 | 29.60 |
| bakery line cut (a dark bakery) | 1.81 | -15.00 | 0.00 | 15.00 |
| main feeder cut (bakery, workshop, market, shed dark) | -11.55 | -15.00 | 0.00 | 15.00 |
| homes cable cut (a dark homes street) | 69.38 | 52.57 | 20.54 | 29.60 |

**Wages stay as they are (owner decision: unfilled places unpaid), and the effect is the OPPOSITE of "cheap" for a dark SHOP street.** A power cut does not empty the work places (only a blocked road does: workers can still reach the shop), so a dark bakery keeps paying its 15 a minute of wages while it sells nothing: its net goes from +52.57 to -15.00 a minute. What the rule makes cheap is a road outage (C03b: a blocked road empties the places and wages stop) and a dark HOMES street: cutting the homes cable changes no money at all, because homes have no machine. So "dark-street outages are cheap" is true only for a street of homes, and it is cheap because it is free, which is a gap in this version rather than a feature.

## Caveats (blunt)
- Wages are unchanged: unfilled places are not paid (see the table above for what that means for dark streets).
- A dim or dark HOME costs nothing: homes have no machine and residents spend the same. This is the largest gap in this version.
- Cost scaling is linear and illustrative. Generator upkeep replaces the shed machine's own wage and power in the district totals (they are not counted twice).
- Nothing was run on an iPad or in AR.
