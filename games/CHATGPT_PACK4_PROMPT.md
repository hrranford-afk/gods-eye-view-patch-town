# Prompt for ChatGPT: Round Table Kingdom, making it fun (19 Nov 2026)

Written by the game session for Harry to paste into ChatGPT.

---

Harry played the first full version of Round Table Kingdom last night. His words:

"It's very cool, but I feel like it's a little bit limited the gameplay at the moment. You don't really get much destruction from the catapult. Gameplay is not much fun to it at all, really. Building the bases don't really seem to make any difference to the outcome at all." And today: "I'm not really sure they're making the most of these buildings and stuff, and the different ways to shoot the enemy."

He is right. This is a design problem, not an art problem, and we want your ideas before we build more.

**What exists now (playable on a practice table; real-table AR is built but untested)**
- Two teams of toy mice. Each player places a kit in 2 minutes: walls, a gate, a watchtower, a tent, a workshop, one Marble Lobber. Mice carry and hammer the pieces.
- Three 1-minute rounds. A flag hill in the middle.
- Version 1 rule: most mice on the hill fills your meter. One marble knocked off one plank. Harry found this dull.
- Version 2, being built today: the watchtower is your Keep, and if it falls you lose. A marble knocks off every part it lands near; things collapse when their supports go; holding the hill earns Big Marbles; three ammunition types; four squad orders (hill, raid, guard, mend); a build budget with a brace and a spring trap.
- Every plank, post, rail and tower leg is its own part. No health bars.
- Rules that will not change: nobody dies (mice go dizzy and pop home), toy weapons only, our own world, no copies of existing games, no real brands. A match is about 8 minutes. One player against a robot, or two players on one iPad.

**What we want from you**

1. **Three different whole-game directions**, each on one page. Not three variations of the same thing: three different answers to "what is the fun here?". For each: the 30-second pitch, what the player does every 10 seconds, why building well wins, why aiming well wins, how a match ends, and the one risk that could make it dull. Then say which one you would build and why.

2. **A building roster of about 10 buildings.** For each: its job, what you lose when it falls, what it combines well with, how the enemy attacks it, and its cost if the whole camp has 12 points to spend. We want every building to change how you play, and at least two that are risky.

3. **A weapons and tricks roster of about 8.** Things the launcher fires and things that are not the launcher at all. For each: what it does to parts and to mice, how the other side defends, and why it is funny. Toy-scale and household: marbles, corks, elastic bands, spoons, string, glue, feathers, a hair dryer for wind.

4. **What the player decides before each shot.** Aiming a lob is not enough. Give 3 ideas that make a shot a real choice.

5. **Pictures**, in the warm hand-painted miniature style of your earlier concept art:
   - one battle picture for the direction you would build, showing a camp mid-collapse;
   - one sheet showing the 10 buildings side by side in the blue team's style, each labelled;
   - one sheet showing the weapons and tricks, each labelled.

**Please keep the same honesty as your earlier packs:** label everything as a proposal, not as tested. Say what you are unsure of. If you think version 2 is the wrong fix, say so plainly and say what you would do instead.

**Send back** one ZIP with `DIRECTIONS.md`, `BUILDINGS.md`, `WEAPONS.md`, `SHOT_CHOICES.md`, the three pictures and a short `HANDOVER.md`.
