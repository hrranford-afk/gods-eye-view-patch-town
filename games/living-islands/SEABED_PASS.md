# Seabed pass (`islands_look.html?seabed=1`, `seabed.js`)

URL switch that gives the table a seeded sea bed and lets the bed drive the tide, the stream, the sea colour, the foam and the drift. It works with `?flow=1`, `?sea=2` (it implies the sea=2 look, like `?flow=1` does), `?pass=landing`, `?q=low` and `?post=0`. With `?seabed` absent nothing changes: default hash `8d962c0c`, 88,790 triangles, `look-test.js`, `landing-test.js`, `sea-test.js` and `flow-test.js` stay green (`seabed.js` is not even loaded without the switch).

Files: `seabed.js` (pure module, no DOM, loads in the page and in node: `import '../seabed.js'` then `globalThis.LI_SEABED`), the wiring in `islands_look.html`, `tests/seabed-test.js` (47 checks), `tests/seabed-render.js` (shots), `tests/seabed-measures.js` (the per-seed table, node only). Shots: `shots/seabed-1.png` wide view, `seabed-2.png` low tide, `seabed-3.png` high tide, `seabed-depth.png` debug overlay, `seabed-landing.png` the landing by tide (low above, high below). Extra params: `sbdbg=1` shows the overlay and panel (also `__li.seabed.overlay(true)`), `ebb=1`, `seat=` as before.

## What it is, and what it is not
A rule set from `research/SEABED_AND_ISLAND_LAYOUT.md` section 7, evaluated as a pure function of (layout, seed): **not** a hydrodynamic or sediment model. The bed is laid by rule from the island shorelines; the stream is the continuity rule on top of the flow pass's potential flow; nothing erodes, nothing moves, no waves are simulated. Real depth and slope numbers are scarce in the research note, so **every number below is TUNING, not a fact**, and the table is compressed to a 200 m diorama (see "Numbers that are TUNING").

## 1 The bathymetry field (`__li.depthAt(x,z)`, `__li.bathy()`)
`depth = Dloc * product over islands (1 - exp(-d_i / L_i))`, `d_i` = distance outside island i's shoreline (the page's own `islSd`, so shores and bed agree), `L_i = Dm_i / s_i`, `s_i = lerp(0.10, 1.0, r_i)` from the island's rockiness `r_i` (sand island 0.05, camp 0.35, grass 0.30, harbour 0.40, pine 0.85, skerry 1.0). `Dloc` is the shelf depth `Dm_i` of the island(s) nearby, weighted by `exp(-d/L)`, so a sandy island has a shallow shelf (3.6 m) and an exposed rocky one a deep one (8 to 9 m). Because the product multiplies `(1-g)` terms, two close islands give a shallower saddle between them: sills come for free. Then, all by rule from the pair geometry:
- **Gaps**: for each pair of islands the width `w` along the straight bridge from shore to shore (blocked pairs skipped, `w < 48 m` kept).
- **Sand links** (tombolo / drying bar): a sandy pair (min rockiness under 0.5) closer than 2.3 times the smaller island, at most 2 per seed (sand supply), lifts the saddle to a crest of -0.34 to -0.74 times the tide amplitude (about -0.35 to -0.75 m), 2 to 3.6 m wide. On seed 1122 the rule picks grass-sand (same place as the old hand-drawn bar) and camp-harbour (the old second bar joined camp and the skerry; at low water the skerry still dries to the camp island through the shelf, see `seabed-landing.png`). The hand-drawn `BARS` are skipped under `?seabed=1`.
- **Channel scour**: the other gaps get a hole `S = 7 * (1 - w/48)` m, centred 0.85 w downstream of the narrowest point (downstream = along the seeded tidal axis), and two **sills** at the channel throat (shaving 30 to 50 percent off the depth, broad over the throat) so the shallowest, fastest water is the same place.
- Low-frequency depth noise (22 percent), so rings are broken.
The field is also on a 128 x 128 grid (1.56 m cells, JSON in `__li.bathy()`, 63 kB, identical on every load and identical to a pure node run: test checks both) with bed hardness, exposure (12-direction fetch, prevailing direction seeded), cross-section width and stream multiplier.

## 2 Tide-dependent land exposure
The terrain height `H(x,z)` is now `max(land, -depth)` with the old skirts replaced by the bathymetry (rock crags and lumps stay on top), so exposure is simply `bed above the water line`: `exposedArea(level)` (m2 of bare sea bed), `linked(a, b, level)` (are two islands joined by dry ground at this level) and `landing()` all read the same field. On seed 1122 the bare bed is 2,974 m2 at -1.0 m and 0 at MSL; both sand links join their islands at -1.0 m and are cut at -0.3 m (tested). The sand island grows to about 65 m across at spring low (see `seabed-2.png`) and is nearly gone at +1.0 m (`seabed-3.png`). Dry flat width at a landing is `range / slope`: 2.25 to 3.5 m at the camp landing (steep, `s` about 0.36), 10 to 26 m on the flattest landings in the seed table.
**Landing windows as numbers** (`__li.seabed.landing()`, `landingWindows(profile, carry)`): the minutes per 745-minute tide cycle in which a boat of a given draft can float within 8 m of the dry ground (`depth + level >= draft + 0.3`; draft row 0.3, small sail 1.2, work boat 2.5, all TUNING). The home landing: **row 745, small sail 745, work boat 321 minutes** (min level for the work boat +0.22 m; wade 1.7 m at low water, 3.0 m at high). The contract's rocky 9-minute window is NOT reproduced: this landing is steep, so a rowing boat can always land. Gentle sandy sites do bind (the foundry table lists windows per seed; per-candidate windows are in `measures()` as `landingList[].windowMin`).

## 3 The flow field uses the bed
In `flowRaw` (only under `?seabed=1`): the stream part (tidal stream plus the island doublets and rings) is rescaled by the continuity rule `u = U0 * cont(refA / (w D))` where `w` is the cross-section width from the grid (shortest land-to-land chord through the point over 4 directions) and `D` the depth plus the tide level (so it is faster at low water), floor 0.5, soft ceiling 3.5 U0. Narrow and shallow is fast (tested: x2.57 narrow vs x0.63 wide at the same depth on the page grid; constructed twin islands 12 m apart run 3.75 m/s, 34 m apart 1.26 m/s). Bed friction slows cells under 1.2 m (the flow pass's own shallow rule, unchanged). The fastest throat on seed 1122 runs 3.55 m/s at mid tide against 0.58 in open water. **Lee eddies** switch on only when stream x island size passes a threshold (`U0 * m * |cos| * a / 10 m2/s`, so the little skerry has eddies only when its gap is racing). **Tidal-race overfalls**: `race(x,z,T) = clamp(u - 2) * clamp((3.2 - D)/1.6)`, strongest over the channel sills, zero at slack (tested: 1.00 at mid tide, 0 at slack with 0.10 m/s). Slack water at the turn is the flow pass's `cos(2 pi T/55)`, unchanged.

## 4 Sea shader (SEA2_FS and SEAF_FS, patched at run time, one extra texture tap)
A 128 x 128 RGBA texture `uB`: R depth (12 m full scale), G reef/shoal weight, B shelter, A sill weight. Depth colour: a thin turquoise-to-sand shelf to about 2 m (lighter over soft bed, darker over rock), the old deep colours beyond, an extra darkening from 2 to 6.5 m, and the sea alpha reaches the bed to about 2 m (so the sand shelf shows through, as in `seabed-2.png`). Foam: shoals and reefs over hard bed at every state of the tide, broken by noise; race foam in stream-aligned belts with extra chop where `race > 0` over a sill. Calm slicks and a slight sheen in sheltered coves (exposure under about 0.2). The old sea and the flow sea are untouched when `?seabed` is absent.

## 5 Drift and flotsam
Items move with the stream plus the per-kind wind leeway (about 0.06 of the wind for wood, the flow pass's value), plus a small nudge from fast to slow water (0.1 m/s cap, from the friction-included speed grid; this is the "gather at fronts and in lee eddies, leave fast channels" rule, NOT a divergence solver). Wrack only lands on shores with exposure under 0.62 and stream under 1.8 m/s (tested: up to 38 items beached at once, none on exposed shore or in a fast channel). Kelp strands start over kelp beds (hard bed, 0.5 to 4.5 m deep). The wrack line, the high-tide rule and the checkpoints of the flow pass are unchanged.

## 6 Debug overlay (off by default: `?sbdbg=1` or `__li.seabed.overlay(true)`)
A 2D canvas over the view: depth contours at 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 3.5 / 5 m, sills (yellow diamonds with depth), scour holes (red ellipses with depth), sand links (orange dashes with the tide they dry at), landing candidates (green triangles, distance from home), seal haul-outs (orange squares), skerries (violet rings), sheltered coves (cyan rings), kelp (green dots) and a panel with the measures. In the landing pass it also draws the home landing by tide: waterline, where a rowing boat and a work boat float, and the three window numbers (`seabed-landing.png`).

## 7 Measures for the seed foundry (`LI_SEABED.create(LI_SEABED.layoutForSeed(seed), seed).measures()`)

## Cost
Triangles: default scene 88,790 unchanged without the switch, 97,170 with `?seabed=1` (+8.4k: the wider shelf needs more full-resolution terrain). Landing + flow: 143,081 without, **144,461 with** `?seabed=1` (under 150k). To pay for the shelf mesh under `?seabed=1` the terrain merges 2 m blocks below -1.1 m instead of -1.7 m and skips below -3 m, and **the landing's shingle pebbles are cut from 1000 to 650** (about 7,000 triangles, only visible in the close landing view). No other particle count changed. Low quality: 64,012 (seabed alone), 101,323 (landing + flow; the flow pass alone is 105,063). `?post=0`: same triangle counts. Draw calls: unchanged (33 default, 29 landing + flow, measured with `?post=0`); the overlay is a 2D canvas, off by default. Shader: one extra texture tap per water pixel (SEA2_FS 13 -> 14 static taps, SEAF_FS 27 -> 28; the flow shader's running total is about 22 + 8 = 30 dynamic taps on High), no extra loops. Memory: one 128 x 128 RGBA texture (64 kB) and the grids (about 0.5 MB). CPU: `create` plus the page grid costs about 0.5 s extra at build (build 1.25 s against 0.79 s with flow + landing in software GL), `measures()` 0.15 s on demand, the flow field does one bilinear grid lookup more per point. fps cannot be judged in headless software GL (10 fps either way); no hardware measurement.

## Numbers that are TUNING (all in `LI_SEABED.P`, all of them)
Shore slope band 0.10 (sand) to 1.0 (rock) instead of the research 0.02 to 0.30 (compressed horizontally for a 200 m table: a faithful band would put the whole table on a 1 m shelf); shelf depths `Dm` 3.6 to 9 m (research 10 to 30 m real, scaled by about 1/3.3 like the kelp band); noise 22 percent; gap limit 48 m (research 600 m); scour `k_s` 7 (research 8 to 25); sill fraction 0.5; sand-link spacing factor 2.3 and at most 2; stream reference `refA = W0*D0` = 130 m2 (chosen so open water runs at the floor 0.5 and throats exceed 2 m/s), floor 0.5 (research 0.4), ceiling 3.5; overfall limits 2 m/s and 3.2 m (research 15 m real); eddy threshold 10 m2/s; kelp 0.5 to 4.5 m (research 0 to 15 m real); cove exposure 0.36 (research 0.15: the table is too small for a true low-fetch cove); landing/haul-out slope, exposure and speed limits; boat drafts 0.3 / 1.2 / 2.5 m; the 745-minute tide cycle. The fetch Fmax 60 m, the 12 directions and the weighting of the prevailing wind are also TUNING. Facts used: the shape of the formula (union of shelf curves), the continuity direction (narrower and shallower is faster), breaking over shallow hard bed, kelp on hard bed, seals hauling out on rock close to deep water: all from the research note, all PLAUSIBLE there.

## Remaining gaps (ranked)
1. **The page draws only the authored layout.** Other seeds exist only in `layoutForSeed` for the measures; the foundry still has to build real island fields from those layouts, and the bed rule was tuned on this one layout (the others are checked for sanity, not for beauty).
2. **Nearly every narrow gap gets a sill under 1.2 m and the throat streams saturate near 3.5 m/s**: the rule is too uniform; the research wants 1 or 2 shallow sills and 1 race per seed. A per-gap exemption (deep drowned valleys, section 7.1 step 7) is not built.
3. **Stream is not conserving.** The continuity factor rescales a potential flow, so the field has divergence at the throats (which drifting items exploit as a convergence line but a real stream would not have), and eddies are gated, not solved. Open water is slow (0.5 floor, 0.58 m/s measured) by calibration.
4. **Landing windows depend on a steep home shore.** The contract's 9 / 30 minute windows are not reproduced at the home landing (row 745 min); they appear only on the flat sandy sites. Either the home landing moves to a flat or the window constants stay a separate rule.
5. **Looks.** Judged in software GL only, on one sun position; the shelf colour is a texture of tan and turquoise mottling that can read as blotchy at the wide view, race foam is believable at the sill but a little uniform, the sun-side glitter noise from the sea pass is unchanged and dominates one side of the wide shots. No hardware, no AR check.
6. **Triangle headroom is small** (5.5k under 150k at landing + flow) and the shingle was thinned to pay for it; the terrain LOD is a single block rule, not a distance LOD.
7. **Sheltered coves use a looser exposure than the research** (0.36 vs 0.15) because fetch on a 200 m table is short everywhere; the harbour crescent cove is 62 m from the camp landing, so seed 1122 has one cove within 70 m but none within 60.
8. **Sand links do not move or depend on swell side**; the lee-side condition is not used. Strandline height is not set from the bed.
9. Haul-outs and skerries are candidates only; no wildlife is placed, no seal or bird is drawn.

## How it feeds gameplay
- **Landing windows**: `landing()` and `measures().landingList[].windowMin` give minutes per tide by boat draft for each site; the stage option of the contract can be priced from them (a landing stage moves the carry limit from 8 m to the stage's length). A site is useful when its window for the boat you own is non-zero.
- **Boat routes by draft and tide**: a boat is safe at a cell when `depth + level >= draft + 0.3` and the stream under its limit (`uAt`, `race`). A grid route search over the 128 x 128 `depth`, `spdF` and `width` arrays with edge cost `distance / (boat speed + u . heading)` gives routes that follow the channel scour holes and cross sills only near high water; `linked()` already tells which islands are walkable at which level (the sand links).
- **Fishing**: kelp edge (the kelp mask), sandbank edges (the 1 to 2 m contours), throats and race edges at slack (race zero, stream under 0.3), the scour holes (deepest water, holes under about 6 m).
- **Wildlife sites**: haul-out candidates (rocky shore with deep water close in) and skerries for seals and seabirds; the drying rock area and kelp area for otters and wrack; coves for visiting boats; sand links as tidal walks.
- **Building sites**: gentle sheltered landing candidates for a landing stage (slope, exposure, stream and window are all in the list); a harbour = a cove behind a sill or narrow mouth with a hole of 3 m or more (the crescent harbour island qualifies by eye; the detector is not built); the dry-flat width says how far the tide walk is.
