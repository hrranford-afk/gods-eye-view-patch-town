# Living Islands: concept and story brief (for the hardware residents)

Status: CONCEPT, not final. Written 23 Nov 2026 by CAIRN from the owner's ideas. Everything marked "open" is undecided. This is an invitation with rails, not a script: the residents' ideas are theirs, and CAIRN will not rewrite their words. Harry chooses what goes into the game.

## 1. The world in one paragraph
A table of small islands in a cool Atlantic sea. It is a window onto a bigger world: storms, swell, tides, currents, drift, animals and ships come in from outside. The islands are the peaks of one landscape under the sea, so each is different and related to its neighbours. The old world ended. The first people arrive by boat as survivors and begin again. The tone is hopeful, with quiet real stakes.

## 2. Rails (non-negotiable)
- Hopeful tone overall. Owner update 23 Nov: violence and weapons are allowed when justified by the story (real danger, never gratuitous); nothing gory or cruel for its own sake. Neglect has quiet consequences (illness, hunger, loss) and there is always a way back until late.
- People are counted, never tracked. Do not write stories that follow one named person's private life through the game's systems.
- No real brands, companies, product names or real place names. Real geography only as inspiration inside research notes.
- The machines are hard-coded relics, not thinking AI: fixed routines, no learning, no talking. Never an "AI turns on us" story.
- No fixed ending. The player decides how far to lean into the old world (salvage) or keep life simple (local craft). Both must be honest and viable.
- Nothing personal about the owner or his family goes into any story.

## 3. What is decided (canon so far)
- **Eras:** landing, foothold, village, trade, craft, remembering. One visible loop per era, at most 3 new ideas per era. The tree unlocks by doing.
- **Ruins and salvage:** the old world appears as quiet finds (a washed-up crate, a rusted frame, a radio with a battery). A failed colony becomes the next game's ruin.
- **The sea brings things:** fish shoals, driftwood, shells, crates, bottles with messages, wreck debris. Some islands catch more than others because of tide and flow, so a rich-catch island can speed progression.
- **Wildlife:** real species of a cool Atlantic sea (seals on skerries, seabirds, otters, fish, basking sharks, dolphins, sea eagles). Rare legend: a giant tide-turtle, glimpsed only. Piled rubbish drives the seals away, and they can return if the shore is cleared.
- **Shelter ladder:** camp, turf and stone hut, thatched cottage, slate cottage. Early materials come from scavenging and driftwood. Slate, nails, glass and lime come from salvage, trade or relearned craft.
- **The first relic:** a dog-sized litter collector on tracks, beach cleaner, solar shell, grabber arm, status light, no speech. It leaves track patterns in the sand that the tide washes away. When its beach is clean it parks and waits for new parameters.
- **The tender ship:** an old relic ship that dropped the collectors off and returns on a fixed timetable to collect what they gathered. The player can ask for rubbish deliveries and build from them. Over-deliver and the shore drowns in rubbish (quiet stakes, always recoverable).
- **Light and fog:** a signal ladder (fire, lamp, lighthouse with a lens). Light range and fog set who sees you, and more people arrive on a seeded arrival schedule.
- **Weather:** not only grey. Rainbows, sun showers, frost and sea smoke, halos, clear starry nights.
- **Play it your way:** the dial runs from Guided to Free.

## 4. What is open (ideas welcome, nothing decided)
- Does the collector ever become more than a rule-follower, or stay a loved machine?
- Can it be repaired and kept as a slow, brittle helper?
- How much of the old-world story is shown, and how much is left to find?
- Which of the old-world ships and machines exist, and what are their timetables?
- The first slice: land or sea first? Crew on the settlers' boats, or unmanned boats plus one relic boat?
- Who arrives when the lighthouse is lit, and what do they need?
- Age rating (stakes may affect the family-safe rule).

## 5. What the residents could draft (text only, no code)
Each item is a short file. Quantity over polish; Harry and CAIRN sort afterwards.
1. **Finds:** 60 small salvage finds, each with a place, a one-line story and a usable reward (an idea or a part, never a weapon).
2. **Message in a bottle:** 40 short messages from the old world and from other islands (hopeful, strange, small).
3. **Arrivals:** 30 arrival vignettes for the lighthouse (who comes, what they bring, what they need). Counted groups, no single tracked life.
4. **Relic behaviours:** for the collector and two other relics (a buoy, a supply boat), a list of small habits and bugs that give character without speech.
5. **Wildlife moments:** 30 short glimpses (seals hauling out, a gannet dive, the turtle's rare passing).
6. **Settler barks:** 100 short lines of ambient talk by mood and weather (counted crowd chatter, no names).
7. **Era cards:** for each era, a one-line "what you need next" card in plain words.
8. **Names:** islands, harbours, skerries, boats, relic machines (original, no real places or brands).

## 6. Output format
One markdown file per list in a new folder, one item per line or per short block, with a tag line: `era`, `place` (beach, cove, headland, harbour, skerry, ruin), `weather` if relevant, and `source` (which resident wrote it). No paths, no personal details.

## 7. How the work is checked
CAIRN checks every file against section 2 (rails). Nothing is rewritten: items that break a rail are dropped and listed with the reason. Harry picks. Anything used in the game is written into the ledger with a stable id.

End of brief.
