# House bill of materials: small coastal cottage and outbuildings (research note)
Do not put real place names into game code, assets or recipes.
Status: research note for Living Islands (branch citylab/cloud-islands). Dated 22 Nov 2026. Nothing here is a recipe.
Method: real facts -> numbers and ranges -> original fictional buildings. No datasets were downloaded; pages were only read (web search summaries and page text).

## 0 How to read the confidence tags
- VERIFIED: stated in a source I could see, with the URL given.
- PLAUSIBLE: my own arithmetic from stated dimensions, or standard trade knowledge I could not tie to one page. Fine for game ranges. Check before quoting.
- UNVERIFIED: a guess or a recollection with no source seen. Treat as a placeholder.
Honest summary: very few figures are VERIFIED. Most quantities below are my own sums (shown in section 2) and are tagged PLAUSIBLE. Labour figures are all PLAUSIBLE or UNVERIFIED: I found no source giving person-days for a whole cottage.

## 2 Four fictional building types (orders of magnitude)
All dimensions are my invention for sizing, not a copied plan. Sums are shown so the owner can re-run them. Every row is PLAUSIBLE unless stated.

### Type A: stone cottage, slate roof (the "full" house)
Shape: about 7.0 x 5.5 m outside, walls 0.6 m thick, 2.4 m to eaves, 40 degree pitch, one chimney stack on a gable, two rooms.
- Wall volume: perimeter along wall centre about 22.6 m x 2.4 m + gables about 10 m2 = about 64 m2 face; x 0.6 m = 38 m3; less door, 3 windows, chimney flue = about 33 to 36 m3 built wall.
- Of that, stone about 25 to 30 m3 solid (about 65 to 80 tonnes at 2.5 to 2.7 t per m3); mortar and pinnings about 25 percent = 8 to 10 m3 wet mortar.
- Lime: about 2.5 to 3 m3 of lime putty (mortar 1:3 to 1:2.5, plus swelling on slaking, section 1). Order of magnitude: a few tonnes of limestone and about a tonne of fuel to burn it (PLAUSIBLE; historic fuel ratio is only one source).
- Foundation: shallow trench of large boulders, about 22 m run x 0.8 x 0.4 = about 7 m3 included in the stone above.
- Roof area: rafter length about 3.4 m x 2 slopes x 7.2 m = about 49 m2.
- Slates: 49 m2 x 19 to 21 per m2 x 1.05 wastage = about 1,000 to 1,100 (Welsh-size); small local slates at 30 to 40 per m2 would be 1,500 to 2,000. Weight about 1.3 to 1.7 t.
- Roof timber: rafters 24 at 3.5 m x 100 x 50 mm = 0.4 m3; wall plates, collars, ridge, purlin = 0.2 to 0.3 m3; battens about 200 m run = 0.1 m3. Total roof about 0.8 to 1.2 m3 sawn softwood. Floor, doors, stair, window frames add 1 to 2 m3.
- Slating nails: 2 per slate = about 2,000 to 4,000 (copper); framing nails or pegs: a few hundred.
- Glass: 3 windows at about 0.55 m2 = about 1.5 to 2 m2 in small panes.
- Door: 1 plank door, 3 hinges and a latch (iron).
- Chimney stack: about 1 to 1.5 m3 stone plus 4 to 6 flue stones or a few dozen bricks/clay pots; lime mortar.
- Finish: limewash 2 to 3 coats over about 70 m2 outer face; a hot-lime or lime putty bucket per few m2.
- Labour (PLAUSIBLE, my estimate; no source): quarry and haul stone 40 to 70 person-days; mason 25 to 40 (about 1 m3 of wall per mason-day with a labourer); lime burning and slaking 10 to 20; carpenter 20 to 30; slater 6 to 12; plaster and limewash 6 to 10; smith (nails, hinges) 3 to 6. Total about 120 to 200 person-days.
- Local on an island: stone (certainly), sand (usually), turf and clay, driftwood or small local timber, peat or wood fuel for burning lime if limestone exists. Shipped: slates if no slate quarry, sawn softwood if no trees, glass, iron, copper nails, limestone or quicklime if the island has none, linseed oil.

### Type B: low thatched stone cottage (the "turf and rope" house)
Shape: about 8 x 5 m outside, walls about 0.9 to 1.0 m thick (double skin, earth or clay fill), 1.7 m high, roof pitch about 45 degrees, hearth in the middle or a gable fireplace.
- Wall: perimeter about 24 m x 1.7 m x 0.9 m = about 37 m3 built; stone about 20 to 25 m3 plus earth/clay fill about 10 m3 (VERIFIED qualitative: earth mortar, turf cap).
- Roof area about 55 to 60 m2. Thatch 250 to 330 mm thick. Bundles: 10 to 12 per m2 (UNVERIFIED) = 550 to 700 bundles of reed; straw or heather thatch will be different and I found no figures.
- Ropes or nets and anchor stones around eaves: some tens of metres of rope, 10 to 20 stones (VERIFIED qualitative).
- Roof timbers: short couples, 6 to 10 pairs, 1 to 2 m3, often driftwood or bog wood. Fewer nails needed, lashings and pegs instead.
- Labour (UNVERIFIED): wall 40 to 60 person-days (earth mortar is quicker than lime); roof frame 10 to 20; thatching one thatcher plus helper about 10 to 20 days for 55 m2 (assuming 3 to 6 m2 a day, a guess); gathering and carrying thatch 30 to 60 days. Total about 100 to 180 person-days.
- Local: nearly all of it, apart from long timbers. Shipped: nothing essential; iron and glass optional.
- Life: thatch needs re-doing every 20 to 40 years (PLAUSIBLE).

### Type C: simple timber outbuilding (store, net shed, boat shed)
Shape: 4 x 3 m, boarded walls 2 m high, pitched roof, post-and-plate frame on stone pad stones.
- Pad stones: 8 to 10 flat stones = about 0.3 to 0.5 m3.
- Frame: 6 to 8 posts 100 x 100 mm, rails and plates 75 x 50 mm: about 0.3 to 0.5 m3.
- Boards: walls about 28 m2 at 20 mm = 0.6 m3 (board and batten adds 0.15 m3); roof boards 15 m2 x 20 mm = 0.3 m3. Total sawn timber about 1.5 to 2.2 m3.
- Roof cover options: tarred board and batten (cheapest), timber shingles (about 60 to 100 shingles per m2, UNVERIFIED), turf, or 300 to 400 slates on battens.
- Nails or pegs: 800 to 1,500 nails (PLAUSIBLE: about 100 per m2 of boarded wall and roof). Door: 1 plank door, 2 strap hinges and a hasp.
- Paint: tar or oil-and-pigment; roughly 10 to 15 litres of tar for 45 m2 of boards at 0.25 to 0.35 l per m2 per coat (UNVERIFIED).
- Labour: felling/sawing 10 to 20 person-days if pit-sawn (UNVERIFIED), carpentry 6 to 10, roof 3 to 5, tarring 1 to 2. Total about 20 to 35 person-days.
- Local vs shipped: boards are the big item. If the island has few trees, boards and nails are shipped. Pad stones, turf and tar from local resin are local only if conifers exist.

### Type D: stone boat store / net loft with slate roof
Shape: 6 x 4 m outside, walls 0.5 m, 2.7 m high to the eaves, one big door, two small windows, loft floor.
- Wall: perimeter about 18 m x 2.7 m = 49 m2 less openings about 6 m2 = 43 m2 x 0.5 m = about 21 m3; stone about 16 to 18 m3; mortar about 4 to 5 m3 (lime or lime/earth).
- Roof about 30 to 35 m2: slates about 600 to 800 (Welsh size) or tarred boards; timber about 0.7 to 1.0 m3 plus loft joists and floor 1.0 to 1.5 m3.
- Labour: about 70 to 110 person-days (stone 20 to 30 mason, 25 to 40 quarry/haul, carpentry 12 to 20, roof 4 to 8, other 4 to 8).
- Local vs shipped: as Type A but fewer slates and no chimney; large door timber and lintel stone are the hard single items.

## 4 Build sequence in plain words (PLAUSIBLE; general trade knowledge)
1. Site and foundation: mark out, dig down to firm ground or rock, lay big boulders as a footing slightly wider than the wall. Stock stone nearby. Timber sheds skip this and use pad stones.
2. Walls: masons build in lifts (a metre or so a day) because wet lime mortar needs time to set. Two faces of larger stones with small stones packed between. Door and window frames and lintels go in as the wall rises. Wall plate (a timber on top of the wall) tied down last.
3. Frame and roof timbers: pairs of rafters (couples) raised, tied with collars, ridge and purlins fixed. For a timber shed, the frame goes up first and the boards follow.
4. Roof cover: battens, then slates from the eaves up with each row lapping the one below, or thatch laid in courses from the eaves up and pinned and roped.
5. Chimney stack and flue: built with the gable wall; capped and flashed in lime or lead.
6. Finish: doors and window frames fitted, glass puttied in, floor laid, plaster or limewash inside and out (two or three thin coats, weeks apart), tar or paint on timber. The walls should dry for a season before heavy plaster.
7. Upkeep: re-point lime mortar every few decades, re-tar timber every 2 to 5 years (UNVERIFIED), re-thatch every 20 to 40 years.
