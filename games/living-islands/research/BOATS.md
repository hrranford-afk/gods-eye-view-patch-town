## 0. The five things that decide whether a boat looks right
1. Sheer: the top edge rises at bow and stern; never a straight line.
2. Freeboard and draft: the hull sits IN the water with 0.3 to 0.5 m freeboard amidships and a dry inside.
3. Laps: each plank overlaps the one below; you see a stripe of shadow under every plank edge.
4. Raked, pointed stem and stern with a fine bow (double-ender) or a small raked transom.
5. Wear: no boat is clean. Tar, faded paint, scuffs at the waterline, a worn rubbing strake.

## 2. Construction
Clinker (lapstrake): planks overlap along their lower edge, fastened with copper rivets (a nail through both planks, a rove (a small washer) put on the inside and the end peened over). Square roves older, round since about 150 years ago (VERIFIED from snippet). Planking is traditionally larch, pine or cedar on oak frames (the "pine/larch on oak" pattern is VERIFIED from a Danish museum source; oak on oak also seen). Frames are steamed oak ribs added after the planking shell (VERIFIED). Gunwale lies on top of the frame heads (VERIFIED from one build page).

The cross-section (midships): a skiff has a shallow V to round bottom with a small keel, flare out about 10 to 20 degrees above the waterline, widest at about 0.45 L from the bow. The fuller hull sections are forward in a rowing boat; the stern runs out fine. The waterline shape is an almond, not an oval.

## 4. How boats sit and move
Hull-in-water facts (all for the visual fix):
- The hull is watertight. The sea outside has a flat level; the SEA PLANE MUST NOT show inside the hull. Inside, the floor is DRY with a small bilge pool at the lowest point of the keel line. Real working skiffs carry a hand's depth (a few centimetres to 5 cm) of water slopping under the floorboards, with a puddle of rain at the lowest point. Never a level equal to the outside waterline. Only a boat swamped or holed has the sea inside at the same height as outside. PLAUSIBLE (physics; certain).
- Waterline: loaded skiff with 2 people sits so that the freeboard (water to gunwale) amidships is 0.25 to 0.35 m and 0.4 to 0.5 m at the bow, less at the stern. TUNING (the inner depth 0.5 m minus draft 0.15 to 0.25 m).
- Trim: slightly stern down with a rower; the bow is lifted. Heel: a few degrees when crew shifts; sailing heel 5 to 15 degrees, 20+ is too much for an open boat. TUNING.
- Riding a wave: a small boat follows the wave slope, pitching about its middle; at 0.5 m waves a 4 m skiff pitches by 5 to 10 degrees, rolls 3 to 5 degrees. The period is short (about 2 to 3 s). A 9 m boat pitches slowly (3 to 4 s). TUNING.
- Wake: rowing at 2 to 3 knots makes a very faint low V and two small eddies (puddles) behind each oar stroke; sailing at 4 to 7 knots: a clear bow wave, a small curl at the stern, a V whose half-angle is about 19 degrees (the Kelvin wake angle, VERIFIED physics). A moored boat shows only a ripple at the bow into the current, a "wedge" of slack on the lee side. PLAUSIBLE.
- Speeds: rowing 2 to 4 knots (hull speed is about 1.34 x sqrt(waterline length in feet) knots: a 14 ft waterline gives about 5 knots); sailing 3 to 7 knots on small boats; gig racing 6 to 8 knots for short bursts; motor boats 6 to 10 knots (displacement boats). VERIFIED rule, PLAUSIBLE numbers.
- Launching over shingle: draft 0.15 to 0.25 m means a skiff floats in 0.3 m of water. In SEABED draft rules (depth + level >= draft + 0.3), a skiff needs only 0.5 m. At low tide the boat is carried or dragged above the strand for up to 30 to 60 m on a gentle shore. TUNING.
- At rest on a beach: the keel is on shingle, the hull propped by stones or short posts or leaning slightly, bow to the sea, often on 2 to 4 round skids (rollers). The boat rests on its keel and the garboard; the bilge does not touch the ground in a double-ender. Heel 3 to 10 degrees. Oars lie inside along the thwarts; mast and sail lashed. PLAUSIBLE.
- Moored at a jetty: two lines (bow and stern) at 30 to 60 degrees to the quay, or the painter at 20 to 35 degrees pulled by wind/current; fenders at the contact line; the boat lies parallel to a stage when tide is slack, swings round at turn (see FLOW_TYPES). On the tide the line goes slack and tight; the bow points into the stronger of wind or current. PLAUSIBLE.
- Rain and spray: water collects in the lowest bilge amidships, behind the floors; the floor has a dark wet area at the middle and white stains of dried salt; a few cm only. Rain lands as drops; it does not "fill" a boat in the game (no sim).

Hull as lofted station rings (as li_camp.rowboat: 22 stations x 9 rings = about 400 quads per skin side). For the lapstrake: each plank = a separate strip with its lower edge pushed 12 to 20 mm outward (real overlap), or one hull mesh with a "lap" step in the normals for T2 and below. Plank texture alone, with no overlap, fails at low sun angles (see mistakes).

What reads at distance (rank): 1 sheer line (the curve at the bow and stern, 2 degrees of rise at 40 m is enough), 2 colour of the hull against the water, 3 the thwart bars (light on dark), 4 the sail or the oars, 5 stem and stern posts, 6 people.

LOD plan: LOD0 under 15 m from the camera (planks, ribs, thwarts, rowlocks, oars, ropes); LOD1 15 to 60 m (one hull with a stripe texture and thwarts; oars as 2 prisms); LOD2 over 60 m (a hull wedge, a mast line, a sail quad; no people). Instance by type; one material per type; 4 colour variants.

Hull-mask mesh for water exclusion: a separate closed mesh built from the OUTER hull and the INNER hull surface, extruded up to the gunwale; used as a depth-only (stencil or alpha) mask so the sea does not draw inside it. The mask is the interior volume (below the gunwale line, inside the inner skin); write depth with the mask first, then the sea shader tests against it. The Blender rowboat has outer + inner shells and a gunwale cap, so the interior polygon is available from its inner shell. Add the dry floor at 0.06 to 0.08 m above the keel line (a flat plane with a dark bilge texture in the middle).

## 8. What is faked
- No hydrodynamics: draft and trim are set values; the pitch and roll is a sine on the wave height at the bow and stern.
- Planks are instance geometry (the lap step) plus a normal map; no real wood thickness at LOD2.
- Water never enters a boat; the hull-mask hides the sea plane; the bilge puddle is a texture.
- The wake is a decal strip (V) following the path; not a computed field.
- Boat counts and types are scaled by village size; individual boats are not tracked beyond owner type.
- Launch and beach scenes use fixed positions on slope and a tide window; no skid animation (a fade or short drag).
- Autonomous craft follow splines.
