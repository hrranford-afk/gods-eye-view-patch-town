# The Gift: story sources (public-domain books and plays, patterns, beats, rights)

Status: research note for the owner. Written 24 Nov 2026. Plain words. Not legal advice.
Confidence tags: HIGH (two or more sources agree, or it is a hard rule), MED (one source, or standard knowledge not re-read), LOW (my memory only, or sources disagree).
Honest limit: every web claim below rests on SEARCH SNIPPETS only. No full page was opened. Author death years not shown in a snippet come from my own memory and are tagged MED. Nothing here was checked by a lawyer.
Rails kept: hopeful tone, nothing gory, robots are relics with no faces or speech, people are counted not tracked. Real names appear only in this note. No page, code or asset was touched.

Rules used for status (HIGH):
- UK: literary and dramatic works last until 70 years after the end of the year the author died. Source: [National Archives copyright guidance](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/terms-and-conditions/copyright/copyright-and-related-rights/) (snippet).
- US: works published in 1930 or earlier are public domain from 1 Jan 2026 (95 years). My brief said "before 1930"; the cut-off has moved by one year. Source: [Duke Public Domain Day 2026](https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2026) and [Emory note](https://scholarblogs.emory.edu/woodruff/news/works-from-1930-enter-the-us-public-domain) (snippets). The 1931 works follow on 1 Jan 2027.
- Translations are separate works. The translator's death year and the translation's publication year decide the translation's status.

## 2 What each book offers as PATTERNS

Never characters, names or text. Tags: avenue (H holiday, S sanctuary, HS homestead, W workshop of repaired old things, D dig-in refuge). Attitude: T trust, X distrust, I investigate, Sh share. Outcome: bad (goes wrong), good (slowly works), mid (in between).

**The Mysterious Island (best fit)**
- A chest of tools, seed and supplies turns up that nobody ordered. Suits HS, W; T, I; good.
- A rescue or fix happens when needed and nobody is seen. Suits all; I, T; good or mid.
- A small group starts with nothing and builds step by step: shelter, fire, water, fields, a workshop. Suits HS, W; T, Sh; good.
- Slow discovery that a hidden helper exists. The helper is never fully explained. Suits all; I; mid.
- A final act that leaves the group safe but changed. Suits HS, S; Sh; good or mid.
- The helper is kind, tired and old. For us the "old machine" is a relic, never a person.

**Robinson Crusoe**
- The wreck gives a one-off haul of useful goods. Rescue what you can before the tide takes it. Suits HS, D; T; good.
- A first harvest fails or is eaten. Try again, with notes. Suits HS; Sh, I; mid.
- Keeping an honest count of goods, good and bad (the famous two-column ledger idea). Suits all; I; any outcome. (The ledger idea is a pattern; do not copy the wording.)
- A fortified home and a second hidden home. Suits D; X; mid.
- A long quiet year, then a footprint-style sign that someone else is near. Use as "a mark on the sand". Suits S, D; I; mid.

**The Swiss Family Robinson**
- A family or crew divides jobs by skill. Suits HS, W; Sh, T; good.
- A tree home first, then a better one. Suits HS, D; T; good.
- Every wreck find is turned into a tool. Suits W; I; good.
- Cheerful setbacks. Suits H, HS; any; good. Watch for colonial and animal-harm scenes. Use none.

**The Tempest**
- An island run by a quiet unseen power (the "spirit" who does the work). Suits all; X, I; mid.
- Visitors arrive by sea after weather and are judged. Suits H, S; Sh, X; mid.
- The keeper gives up control at the end. Suits all; Sh; good.
- Avoid the servitude and cruelty. For us, the helper is a relic and nobody is a captive.

**News from Nowhere**
- A society that makes things well, mends things, and likes the work. Suits W, H; T, Sh; good.
- Old buildings kept and reused. Suits W, S; T; good.
- Money is hardly used. A gift economy. Suits all; Sh; good.
- A visitor wakes in a future and is told little. Suits all; I; good.
- The ending: a good place that cannot be held onto. Gentle sadness. Suits all; mid.

**Walden**
- One year of careful accounts for a small house and bean field. Suits HS; I; good.
- Small, slow, chosen life as a quiet rebellion. Suits HS, S; X, T; good.
- Visitors, then silence. Suits S; any; good.
- Seasons as the clock.

**The Coral Island**
- Three friends, a bay, a pearl-like find, a cave, pigs and fruit. Suits H, S; T, Sh; good. (Skip the fights and the missionary part.)

**Utopia**
- An island with a ring of harbours, shared work, and a plain way of keeping stock. Suits S, H; T; good.
- Visitors from a far shore are treated oddly kindly. Suits H; Sh; good.
- A narrator who is half sure it is a joke. Suits all; X; mid. This is the best "doubt" mood in the list.

**New Atlantis**
- A hidden island institution quietly sends people out to gather knowledge and sends gifts back. Suits all; T, I; good.
- Strangers arrive in distress and are looked after for three days without questions. Suits H, S; Sh, T; good. Strong fit for the Gift itself.
- A founding house of experiments and inventions. Suits W; I; good.
- Short text, calm mood, all PD.

**The Machine Stops (patterns only; text not free in UK)**
- People in comfort are fed by an unseen system. They stop doing the work. Suits H; T; bad.
- A small voice says the system is failing and nobody listens. Suits all; X, I; bad.
- The system fails slowly: small faults first. Suits all; bad or mid.
- Some people live outside and keep the old skills. Suits HS, D, W; X; good.
- This book is the model for "the world goes wrong" arm. Keep it gentle: faults, delays, thinning credit. Not collapse.

**Erewhon**
- A land that locked away old machines for fear. Suits D, W; X; mid.
- Doubt about the helper, written as comedy. Suits all; X; mid.

**After London**
- Nature takes back farmland. Fields become marsh and wood. Suits S, HS; T; good or mid.
- A traveller crosses a changed land with simple tools. Suits all; I; mid.
- The best source for "slow and quiet". Use the beauty. Skip the cruelty and the caste.

**The Time Machine**
- Gentle, fed people and a hidden system under the ground. Suits H; I, X; mid or bad.
- Use only the mood. Skip the dark half.

**Looking Backward**
- Wake up in a calm, plain, well-run day. Suits all; I; good.
- Shared stores and an everyday card (a credit card idea from 1888). A pattern for "credit". Suits all; T, I; good.

**The Secret Garden**
- A shut-away place, a key, and slow care. Suits S, HS; T, Sh; good.
- Children and animals as the first guests. Counted, not tracked.

**The Count of Monte Cristo**
- A secret treasure and an unseen helper who pays debts quietly. Suits H, HS; T, I; mid.
- Use only the "unseen benefactor" and "island treasure" moods. Skip the revenge.

**Gulliver's Travels, Treasure Island, Great Expectations (thin use)**
- Flying island hung in the sky (Gulliver). Suits late game "cloud island" idea; I; mid.
- A map and a hidden cache (Treasure Island). Suits H, W; I; good. A cache of old things, no violence.
- A gift from a source you do not know (Great Expectations). Suits all; X, I; mid.

**Not recommended as sources:** The Last Man (too grim), R.U.R. (robots speak and rebel; clashes with the relic rule and has UK translation issues), Time Machine's later half.

Count: 28 beats. Spread: avenues all five covered. Attitudes: trust 12, distrust 6, investigate 14, share 6 (some beats carry two). Outcomes: good 20, mid 10, bad 3.

Bad-outcome beats are only 3, so the bad arm needs more beats before it feels natural. Suggested extras (my own, not from the books): a harbour log that stops being filled in; a spare part that no longer exists in the catalogue; a lighthouse that goes dark for a week.

How the beats show up naturally (not by menu): all are events from the weather, delivery list, shore finds and bottle letters. The player never picks "bad world" or "good world". The ledger and the island decide.

## 4 Rights note

Free (HIGH):
- Ideas, plots, patterns and moods are not protected anywhere. A hidden helper, a failed harvest, a tool left behind are free.
- Text, and named and distinctive characters, are free only if the work and the translation are public domain in the place you sell.

How to quote safely:
- Quote only from a Gutenberg or Standard Ebooks text of an old translation. Say which one in a credits file.
- Keep each quote short (one line, ideally under 25 words). Do not stack many from one book.
- Do not quote from a modern translation, modern edition notes, a film, or a play adaptation.
- For anything that is UK-protected (Forster, Selver's translation), use no text in a UK-facing release.
- In our own prose, use our own plain words. Our letters should sound like us, not like Verne.

Avoid:
- Captain Nemo as a named character, and Nemo's name, the submarine's name, and film designs (the 1954 and 1961 films, comics and games are copyright). The book text is free but your brief says no. Keep it that way.
- Character names from any book: no Crusoe's helper's name, no Prospero, Ariel, Caliban, no Cyrus Smith, no Fritz, no Gulliver, no Long John. Use none.
- Famous lines from modern copyright films and games: no "I'm sorry, Dave", no "I'll be back", no "Wilson!" from the castaway film, no robot-voice lines from any film or game. Also avoid a glowing-eye or voiced helper AI (rail: no faces, no speech).
- Brand names in assets. (Real names are only allowed in this note.)

Needs a human or lawyer check:
1. The UK status of Forster's The Machine Stops (we say protected to end of 2040), and whether the owner will sell in the US only, the UK only, or both.
2. The UK status of the Kingston-credited Mysterious Island 1875 translation (who the translator was) and of White's (snippets disagree on Kingston; White's UK status is my arithmetic).
3. UK 25-year typographical-arrangement right on any edition we copy text from.
4. Project Gutenberg's own terms: using its text is free, but using the "Project Gutenberg" name or logo needs its licence. Strip or follow its header. (MED, memory, not checked.)
5. Folger and other modern editions of The Tempest (licence not checked, LOW).
6. Whether any quote is even needed. Safest plan: use none, or one or two lines from White, Defoe, Morris or Thoreau.
7. The Gift name and trademark check, already flagged in the design note.
8. Kravitz and Stump modern Verne translations: confirm they are in copyright (LOW, memory).

## 5 Headline finding

Best three fully safe books (PD in UK and US, plain English texts, easy to use):
1. The Mysterious Island. It is the nearest thing to The Gift: a hidden helper, a tool chest nobody ordered, a group that builds up from nothing, and a helper never fully explained. Use the White translation only.
2. Robinson Crusoe. It gives the beats that feel true to a first year: wreck salvage, failed harvest, honest lists, a long quiet. English original, no translation worry.
3. News from Nowhere. It carries the "slowly works out" and workshop of repaired old things sides, and an easy-going view of money.
Specials: New Atlantis (hidden helper and kind welcome, PD both places, very short) and The Machine Stops (the model for the "goes wrong, slowly" arm, but patterns only, because it is not PD in the UK until 2041). After London gives the gentle-collapse mood. Walden gives the homestead accounts mood.

## 6 Open questions for the owner (taste only)

1. Should the bad arm stay mild (late parcels, thin seasons, a dark lighthouse) or ever go further? Default: mild, as Machine Stops patterns but no collapse.
2. Do you want any quoted line at all in game letters, or only our own words in the spirit of these books? Default: our own words.
3. Is the hidden helper allowed to feel like a gentle old institution (New Atlantis) or should it stay fully faceless and mechanical? Default: an old institution you never meet.
4. Should visitors in distress (beat 13) be a regular event, or rare? Default: rare, once or twice a year.
5. Which attitude do you want rewarded least? Default: none. Distrust, trust, investigating and sharing all find a way.
6. Do you want a credits page naming the books? Default: yes, a small one.
