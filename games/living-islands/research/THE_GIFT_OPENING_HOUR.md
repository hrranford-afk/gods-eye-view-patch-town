# The Gift: the opening hour (script, v0)

Status: draft for the owner. Written 24 Nov. Plain words, short beats. Taste items are marked TASTE.
Built from things we already have: camp mode, the robot yard, the repair jetty, the tender boat, weather moods, the lighthouse, the ledger idea in THE_GIFT_DESIGN.md.
Rails kept: hopeful tone, nothing gory, robots are relics (no faces, no speech, no feelings), people are counted not tracked.

## The idea in one line
You wake on a small island you were given. Deliveries keep arriving. Nobody says why. You choose what to do with it.

## Minute 0 to 3: the arrival
1. Black screen, sound of sea and gulls. A small boat engine fades out.
2. Dawn. A rowing tender leaves the beach. You are on a pebble beach, a wrecked jetty to one side, a rusted hut on the rise. Camp mode: no village yet.
3. A crate sits above the tideline with a paper label and a brass key. Tap it.
4. Letter (one page, no names, handwritten look):
   > "This island is yours. So is what is in the ledger. Spend it slowly. Parcels will come by boat. If something looks old, it is probably still useful. You do not need to understand why."
5. The ledger opens: **£1,000,000**, split in two lines:
   - **Cash £60,000** (spends anywhere, for local things)
   - **Delivery credit £940,000** (only buys deliveries from the catalogue)
6. TASTE: the split. 6 percent cash is my default. Tight enough to matter, loose enough to start.

## Minute 3 to 10: first jobs (the camp)
Three quiet jobs, any order. Each has a hint, never a forced tutorial.
1. **Get dry.** Shelter. Pick from what is on the beach: lean-to kit, tarp, or patch the hut. Weather is already moving in (mood system).
2. **Find the water.** Look at the island: a spring, a rain barrel from the crate, or a well later. Water is real: not every island has a spring.
3. **Look around.** Walk the shore. Finds wash up (the drift table). Seals on the rocks keep their distance. Birds are a food and a charm, never a quest marker.

First small win at about minute 8: you fit one plank of the wrecked jetty (the repair pilot) and the tender can land closer. This is the tutorial for "fix old things".

## Minute 10 to 20: the first delivery
1. A boat appears on the horizon. Fog horn, then the tender, no crew visible (unmanned: it is just a boat that knows the way).
2. It leaves a numbered crate. The catalogue opens: ten cheap things (rope, tools, seed tin, lamp oil, tarps, a hand pump).
3. Order one thing with credit. A delivery marker shows when it will come: tomorrow morning. Time matters: real delivery times, weather can delay.
4. The ledger shows **credit now £938,200**. Cash unchanged. First lesson: credit buys things, not land.

## Minute 20 to 35: the yard and the old machines
1. A mossy yard behind the hut. Three collector robots sit in it, switched off. They look like old rolling carts with arms. No faces, no eyes that look alive.
2. Tap one. A plain label: "Old carrier. Needs a part." The player can swap a worn track from a delivery or from the wreck.
3. A robot starts. It collects tideline rubbish and drops it in a pile. It never speaks and never reacts like a creature.
4. This is where the story whispers: a stamp on its chassis, the same mark as the letter.

## Minute 35 to 50: the first real choice
Evening. The lighthouse stump on the headland shows a faint light.
1. A second letter arrives with the evening delivery:
   > "You can make this a place for people to visit. You can make it a place for wildlife. You can make it somewhere to live quietly. Or you can do all three badly. None of it is wrong."
2. The build menu shows five things (Level 1): jetty, cottage, cistern, wildlife hide, kiosk. Each shows cost, what it earns or protects, and a delivery time.
3. The player builds one. Whatever they choose sets their first year: cottage leads to guests (counted), hide leads to wildlife, cistern leads to staying power.

## Minute 50 to 60: the first night
1. Night falls with the sky clock: stars, moon, tide change. The lighthouse stump light is the only light.
2. A bottle washes up. Message (from the town's bottle messages, one chosen by seed). No hints about the AI yet.
3. The ledger shows a small line at the bottom: "Delivery credit lasts about 4 years at this pace." Soft pressure, not a countdown.
4. Fade out on the island at night. The hour ends with the first save.

## How the story reveals itself (not all in the hour)
- Ledger stamps, the same mark on letters, robots and crates.
- Bottle messages that are slightly too well informed.
- A delivery that arrives before it was ordered (once, in year two).
- Neighbour parcels appear on the chart; some look prepared too.
- The AI never speaks and never gets a face. The player decides what it is.

## The dial: credit thins over the years
- Year 1: full catalogue, fast delivery.
- Year 2: some items slower.
- Year 3: some items vanish from the catalogue.
- Year 4 onwards: only basics. The island must carry itself. Local making (the kit, repair, farming, fishing) takes over.
- Ending is a report card with four lenses (profit, wildlife, happiness, resilience), no single win state.

## Questions for the owner (taste only)
1. Cash/credit split: 6 percent cash? More cash is easier, less is more tense.
2. Does a human ever appear in the first hour? My default: no, only the empty tender and letters.
3. Should the first hour be skippable on replay? My default: yes, after the first save.
4. How much should the AI mark show? My default: small, easy to miss, never explained.
