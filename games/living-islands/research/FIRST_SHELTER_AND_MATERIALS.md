# First shelter and materials: the ladder from a lean-to to a slate cottage (research note)
Status: research note for Living Islands (branch citylab/cloud-islands). Dated 23 Nov 2026. Follows RESEARCH_FIRST.md. Nothing here is a recipe.
Real names appear only in this note. Do not put them into code, assets or recipes.
Method: reuse research/HOUSE_BILL_OF_MATERIALS.md (HBOM) for quantities, add what a colony with almost nothing can actually do first, and tie it to the sea, the rubbish economy and the tender ship. No new web pages were read for this note: every source below was already cited in HBOM or comes from the repo's own notes, and anything new is my own reasoning.

## 0 Confidence tags
VERIFIED = stated in a source seen (HBOM carries the URLs). PLAUSIBLE = my arithmetic or standard trade knowledge. UNVERIFIED = a guess. TUNING = a game design number. Honest summary: apart from the qualitative facts HBOM already verified, this note is PLAUSIBLE, UNVERIFIED and TUNING. Labour figures for the two lowest rungs have no source at all.

## 1 The ladder in one table
| Rung | What it is | Main materials | Tools | Labour (person-days, whole job) | Needs from outside the island |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Camp | lean-to or ridge tent: a ridge pole on two forked posts, a sail or tarp over it, edges weighted with stones, a windbreak of driftwood or stacked turf on the windward side | 4 to 6 driftwood spars, 3 to 4 units of cloth or net or rope (about 10 to 15 m of line), stones | axe, a knife; hands | 1 to 3 (UNVERIFIED; my estimate, nobody wrote this down) | nothing beyond the starting kit |

| 3 Thatched cottage | HBOM type B as written: about 8 x 5 m outside, double-skin wall about 0.9 m with fill, 550 to 700 reed bundles or equivalent heather or straw, ropes and anchor stones | 20 to 25 m3 stone plus about 10 m3 earth fill, 1 to 2 m3 roof timber, tens of metres of rope | as above plus a billhook or sickle, a thatcher's comb and leggett (UNVERIFIED names), a sledge | 100 to 180 (HBOM, UNVERIFIED) | optionally iron and glass; nothing essential |
| 4 Slate cottage | HBOM type A as written: about 7 x 5.5 m, lime-bonded walls 0.6 m, 40 degree roof, one chimney, glass in three windows | 25 to 30 m3 stone, 8 to 10 m3 wet mortar (2.5 to 3 m3 lime putty), about 1,000 to 2,000 slates, 2,000 to 4,000 nails, 1 to 2 m2 glass, 2 to 3 m3 sawn timber | masons' and slaters' tools, a lime kiln, a forge, a saw pit | 120 to 200 (HBOM, UNVERIFIED) | lime (or limestone and fuel), slates, copper or iron nails, glass, long sawn timber, unless found or relearned |

Reading the ladder: labour jumps about 20x between rung 1 and rung 2, then only about 3x to rung 3. Rung 4 is not about more labour than rung 3, it is about things the island cannot make. That break is the point of the design: rungs 1 to 3 are limited by effort and driftwood, rung 4 by trade and know-how.

## 3 The tools the owner starts with
Owner's lean (CAIRN's reading): a new colony lands with a small kit, an axe and a sail, and almost nothing else.
- The axe turns a driftwood log into spars, pegs and a ridge pole, and is the one item that cannot be improvised from the shore (a sharp flint or a scrap-metal blade can, slowly: UNVERIFIED). Without it rung 1 still works with whole spars; rung 2 slows a lot.
- The sail is both the first roof and the first rope: its seams give cord, its edges give a hem for stones. It is the best single object for story (a faded stripe is allowed, no legible writing).
- Everything else is found: a knife blade from the hopper's metal, a spade from a crate lid and a stake (UNVERIFIED), a sledge from planks. This is the scavenging ladder of EVOLUTION_TREE.md: hopper, then a delivery, then a stockpile.

## 4 Scavenging, local craft, trade: how it ties to the rest of the world
- **Shore catch (ecosystem/shore_catch.py).** The strandline gives items per metre per tide (poor 0.2, normal 1, rich 5, TUNING, anchored in BEACH_COLLECTOR section 5). Storms spike it 3 to 10 times and winter runs richer than summer. Here that means: the first storm is the first big timber delivery, and a colony landing in autumn has more wood and less daylight than one landing in spring. A west-facing gentle beach is the best home shore, and it is also where the landing finder puts the village (gentle, lee side), which is a mild tension: the lee beach is poorer. Wood share of the catch is the largest unknown (section 9).
- **The globe's drift arrivals (globe/, GLOBE_STAGE2.md).** Arrivals are keyed `seed:globe:kind:index` with a source region, travel days and a fragility. Tag some of them as timber and cloth: a spar that travelled 300 days is weathered and silver, one from a nearby wreck is fresh. They are the supply that makes a shore rich or poor over the whole game, and a source of the story finds (a crate, a bottle).
- **The rubbish economy and the tender ship.** The litter collector's hopper is a feed of metal, glass, rope, cloth and plastic, not timber (the hopper gets litter, spars are carried by hand). The tender brings 2 to 8 kg a visit normally, 20 kg at most (BEACH_COLLECTOR section 9), which is nails and panes, not slates. So rungs 1 to 3 do not need the tender at all; rung 4 does, and that is where over-delivering to the shore becomes a choice (more metal and glass against a buried landing and seals that leave).
- **Ruins and relearned know-how.** Slates, panes and the idea of a lime kiln come from ruins, once found. A find is claimed once (idempotent key). A failed colony becomes the next run's ruin, so a previous colony's slate cottage is where the next colony's slates come from.
- **City Lab houses.** They are showcase buildings, polished and finished, and show what the ladder is heading for. A new colony does not start with them.

## 5 What a new colony does in the first hour (the loop)
Game hour, not real hour. The kit is an axe and a sail. The visible loop is one card, never a wall of numbers.
1. Land, look round (landing finder has already placed the village on the lee side). Water, fire, food come first as in the Landing era.
2. Walk the strandline at low tide: pick up spars, rope, net, cloth. First tide gives enough for a lean-to (4 to 6 spars, TUNING).
3. Raise the lean-to: ridge pole on two forked posts, the sail over it, stones on the hem, a windbreak. Sleeping out of the wind is the first win and clears the first "tired" card.
4. Light the fire in its lee. Fill the first store with whatever food the shore and the rocks give.
5. Second tide: sort what came in. Wood in one pile, rope and cloth in another, metal and glass in a third, stone near the site. The card says "a hut needs about 14 spars and a month of work" so the next goal is visible.
6. Before dark: mark the hut site on firm, dry ground above the storm line and out of the seals' haul-out.
Stakes (quiet): nothing happens in the first hour that can kill a colony. A bad shelter shows as tired people and a damp bed; a first storm with no lean-to shows as lost cloth and a slow day; neglect over weeks shows as illness and a stalled card, with a way back (a rest day, a skipper, a ration). Loss is a counted number, never a person's story.

## 6 Common mistakes to avoid (so it looks right)
- A tidy log cabin on day one. The first shelter is crooked, low, leaky and made of mixed parts.
- Thatch with no ropes or anchor stones; real roofs here are tied down against the wind (HBOM VERIFIED).
- A slate roof on a turf hut, or glass in rung 2 or 3. Glass belongs to rung 4.
- Driftwood piled neatly. It is bleached, silver, salt-cracked, with rusted bolt holes.
- Lime and cement confusion: hard cement on soft stone damages it (HBOM VERIFIED). Rung 4 uses lime.
- A bright new sail. It is faded, patched, salt-stiff.
- Unlimited timber. Spars are finite, found on the strandline, mostly after storms.

## 7 Visual and behavioural checklist
- Rung 1 reads as a pitched cloth shape with a pole spine and stones on the hem, on a small level patch.
- Rung 2 reads as a low, rounded, mossy mound with a dark doorway and smoke, walls thicker than the door is high.
- Rung 3 reads as a long low roof with a rope net over the thatch, anchor stones at the eaves, tiny windows.
- Rung 4 reads as a crisp gable, a chimney stack, a sharp slate roof, a few small panes, limewash with a pale earth tint.
- Each rung is visibly a better version of the one before it, built beside it, not instead of it. The old lean-to becomes a store.
- Weathering follows age: new thatch is gold and fades grey; limewash goes chalky; driftwood goes silver.

## 8 What is faked
- Quantities are scaled from HBOM and rounded, never counted board by board.
- Labour is a single pool of person-days with a work factor; there are no trades, no skill, no named workers (people are counted).
- Catch is turned into building units by fixed shares (wood, rope and cloth, metal, glass), not item by item.
- Lime, nails and glass are gated by tender calls, not by a real kiln, forge or furnace chain.
- Thatch gathering is folded into the labour for rung 3.
- Weathering of materials over time, rot and re-thatching are not in the model.
- Relearned know-how is a flag from a ruin find, not a research tree.

## 9 Headless model: ecosystem/shelter_ladder.py
Deterministic (python and numpy only, tests in tests/test_shelter_ladder.py, 5 checks pass). It reads shore catch per seed from ecosystem/shore_catch.py (rate per metre per tide, storm and season multipliers, the landing finder) and runs a daily loop.
- **Inputs:** seed; start day of year (spring, 105, TUNING); party size (6, TUNING); a policy.
- **Catch:** each person-day of combing covers 250 m of the best strandline within 600 m of the landing, and finds 60 percent of what arrived (TUNING). Items turn into building units per 100 items: 8 wood, 10 rope and cloth, 4 metal, 6 glass (the last two from the litter shares in collector_econ; the first two TUNING).
- **Labour:** 6 people x 0.7 work factor x a daylight factor, 0.3 of that on storm days (strength 4 or over).
- **Ladder:** camp needs 6 wood, 4 rope, 2 person-days; hut 14, 8, 45; thatched 22, 15, 130; slate 12 wood, 6 rope, 200 metal items, 12 glass items, 160 person-days and 3 tender calls (TUNING, scaled from section 1). The starting sail counts as 3 rope units.
- **Policies:** comb_heavy (60 percent of labour on the shore), build_heavy (20 percent), collector_led (10 percent, and the litter collector fills a hopper that the tender empties). If the next rung is short of materials, everyone combs until it is not.
- **Outputs:** the day each rung is finished (400 day horizon) per policy.

Headline numbers, 60 seeds (seeds 1 to 60 of the existing worlds), start in spring, 6 settlers. All TUNING.
- 22 of 60 seeds have no home shore at all (the basalt province has no beach: every third seed). These are counted apart, not failed: a different game (a boat first). The 38 others:
| Policy | Camp (median day) | Hut | Thatched cottage | Slate cottage (reached) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| comb_heavy | 3 | 30 (28 to 32 for p10 to p90) | 98 (96 to 102) | 200 (193 to 209), 97 percent |
| build_heavy | 2 | 16 (15 to 18) | 52 (50 to 55) | 95 (92 to 99), 97 percent |
| collector_led | 2 | 15 (13 to 17) | 47 (44 to 50) | 84 (82 to 87), 97 percent |
- What it says: with six people, labour decides the ladder, not the catch. The seeds differ little (the p10 to p90 spread is about 10 percent), because the shore only matters when it is very poor (one seed with 13 m of usable strandline reaches rung 3 at day 157 and never reaches rung 4 in 400 days).
- Cutting the wood share from 8 to 2 per 100 items changes the build_heavy medians by only a day. So the headline is not sensitive to the wood guess until it gets very low. At 0.5 per 100 (one spar in 200 items) wood starts to bind: build_heavy medians become hut 19, thatched 56, slate 97 and one seed in 38 fails to reach the hut. A party of 3 was started and not finished, so it is not claimed.
- The tender's three calls (about 30 to 40 days at the seeded 8 to 14 day interval) set a floor of roughly day 40 for rung 4 whatever the labour.
- Danger signs for the design: if the real game lets a player finish a thatched cottage in under 2 months with a party of 6, rung 3 is probably too cheap (HBOM says 100 to 180 person-days for the whole house; the model uses 130 and a party that does 4.2 person-days a day, so about a month of pure building). Treat as a first pass.

## 10 Could not verify (the short list)
- Person-days for rungs 1 and 2: no source; scaled from HBOM which is itself unsourced.
- Share of strandline catch that is usable timber and cloth. Real Atlantic beaches differ widely; a litter-count share is not a volume share.
- How many spars a lean-to and a hut really need, and how long a driftwood spar lasts in a roof (rot).
- Whether burnt shell makes usable lime in the quantities needed, and how much fuel an island without trees has.
- Glass: how a colony relearns it. Not researched.
- Real roof and wall details of historical hut types (HES teaching pages, not opened here).
- Thatch bundle sizes and the gathering rate (HBOM already lists them as unclear).
- What the player's tolerance is for a 400-day ladder against a game clock. Pacing is a design question.

## 11 Biggest unknowns
1. Labour numbers for the first two rungs (nothing sourced).
2. The wood and cloth share of the catch, and whether timber arrival needs its own flow in the globe's drift arrivals.
3. Rung 4 gating: tender calls versus ruin finds versus a trade route.
4. Whether 6 settlers is the right start, and whether the first-hour loop is dull when the catch is not the bottleneck.
