# The Gift: the core loop plan (owner focus, 24 Nov)

Owner said: stick to the core bits. Building. A boat to zip around in. The whirlpool so storms bring stuff. The little robots: a short mission unlocks them, then they collect the right things for building.
One job at a time. Each job ends with something the owner can press and play. Aims are guesses.

## Job 1: First day plus robot orders (running)
Done when: the owner can arrive, read the letter, fix the jetty plank, order a delivery, fit the missing part so a robot starts, give it a collection order (planks, rope, glass, tins), see stock counts rise, build one thing that spends that stock, and sleep through the first night. Saves and loads.

## Job 2: A boat you can zip around in
Done when: the owner can take the small tender from the jetty and drive it round the islands (thumb stick on iPad, keys on desktop), with a believable wake and bobbing on the waves, landing at a beach, carrying a small load (robots' stock or a crate), and a small engine part that makes it faster. Wildlife and weather react. Uses boat_motion.js and the boat kit. Starts as a rowing boat or small found engine; the pole-drive skiff and the tuning shop come later.

## Job 3: Storm and the haul (the whirlpool)
Done when: a storm builds (weather mood), a tidal swirl and surge sweep in, a haul of debris is left along the wrack line (reuse the aftermath debris work: planks, barrels, crates, rope, nets, one hulk later), and the robots can be told to gather it. A storm is foreshadowed by weather. Wreckage and cargo only, nothing gory.

## Job 4: Robot life (after jobs 2 and 3)
Owner point: the robots look plonked on one island with no real thought; they are a key part of the early game and need to move around and do different things. First day already adds: routines (dawn out, work the tideline, dusk charge, night off), visible work steps, a charging post, heading home before storms, two different job behaviours. Job 4 adds: crossing to other islands at low tide, getting stuck and rescue (status light, go and free it), body types with different jobs (hauler, sorter, stacker), working together on big loads, and wear, dust, moss and repainting. Rails: no faces, no speech, no feelings.

## Job 5: Make it physical (owner playtest, 25 Nov; starts after Job 3 lands)
Owner played the first day: it is all happening automatically; things should move around to show it happening; we need to work together to reach each point. Rule: every change on a counter has a visible cause. (1) Deliveries: a boat arrives and robots carry crates up the beach to the yard; no crate just appears. (2) Stock: planks, rope, glass and metal pile up in the yard as heaps you can see, not only a number. (3) Building goes up in visible stages (stakes, frame, walls, roof) with robots and the player working on it. (4) Co-operative jobs: some tasks need the player and a robot together (carry the two ends of a beam; a robot waits at the yard until you bring its part). (5) Gates need the player: the robot starts only after the part is fitted; a build proceeds only when the player has delivered what it needs; the clock alone never advances a gate. (6) Day and night rhythm shows: robots out at dawn, home at dusk, lights on. Hands-on input: a short drag to carry a thing to a spot (default; owner to confirm). Pace reference: the original's measured pace (one load to the heap every 64 s, about 2.4 of 3 robots out) stays. Also: playthrough pass folded in (play the day, list dead ends). Rails unchanged.

## Playtest rules (owner, 25 Nov): apply to every job
1. Every interactive object explains itself: desktop hover highlight and label card (what it is, what it is for, what it needs, the action); touch: first tap opens the card with the action button; highlight classes gold (needs you now), white (interactive), none (scenery); a thing you cannot act on yet does not glow, it shows why. One data table for the labels.
2. The game never leaves the player in an impossible state and always says why something cannot be done (stock numbers, daylight, tide, a missing part).
3. The player can always gather by hand (night: inside the lantern's light); by-hand rate about one robot's rate and needs full attention, so robots set correctly matter; show both rates.
4. No dead stretches over about two game minutes: small visible events and one always-doable 'what next'.
5. A permanent Build button; a clear first destination marker for exploration.

## Job 7: Robot parts and the Parcel Post magazine (after Job 6; owner playtest 25 Nov)
Robots get module slots and the catalogue sells modules (bigger battery, solar panel, hauling arm, sorting tray, builder arm, lamp, tracks or legs, charger); jobs need particular modules; wrong fit means visible downtime; a robot panel shows slots and what each job needs; swapping a part is a short hands-on job (hold the ring, a visible swap). A weekly in-fiction magazine panel arrives with the delivery (third voice, dry and warm, small jokes): new and coming parts with arrival days, items leaving the catalogue, a tip, a neighbour's letter; data in gift/events.json and the catalogue; one paper panel. Every glowing object states what it needs. Keep the pace reference by day and the night shifts from Job 6.
