# Land formation tool v0 (Living Islands, branch citylab/cloud-islands)
Do not put real place names into game code or assets. Status: v0, numpy only (PIL for images). Files: `world.py`, `checks.py`, `sheet.py`, `tests/test_world.py`, `out/` (3 sample exports, manifest, the 20-seed check table), `contact_sheet.png`, `world-1122-{plan,section,tides}.png`.

## 1 The idea
The owner's rule: each island is a different peak of the land under the sea. So the world is ONE height field `h(x,y)` over the whole 512 m region, seabed included (metres, sea level 0, seabed negative). Nothing is placed. Sea level is a plane; islands are the connected components of `h > level`; skerries are the small ones; shelves, sills, channels, sheltered bays and reefs are the same surface below the plane. The tide only moves the cut (`+-1.1 m`, the seabed pass amplitude; `world.TIDE_AMP`): `find_islands(w, tide=...)`, `land_mask(w,'low'|'mid'|'high')`. Each island record carries `area_low_tide_m2`, `joins_at_low_tide` (other islands it merges with) and `pieces_at_high_tide`.

Shared structure is what makes neighbours relate. One `structure` dict (dip direction and angle, ice-flow vector, tilt, a set of fault lines with throw, wave direction) is drawn once per seed and used by every step over the whole field. Province A: knolls are noise stretched along the ice-flow vector, so every whaleback and skerry points the same way, troughs run along the flow with a lip at the mouth, foliation bands and faults shift the rock across the whole world. B: dip-aligned cuesta ridges (steep scarp up-dip, long dip slope) broken into strike-aligned islands, benches (terraces) whose edge sharpness follows rock hardness, faults throwing the beds sideways so ridges are offset across a fault. C: flat-lying trap steps, a dyke swarm of parallel trenches that cuts the seabed too (geos where it meets the sea), stacks and arches on exposed cliffs.

## 2 Pipeline as implemented (`world.make_world`)
Order in code (research numbering): 2 rock map, 1+3 uplift/tilt/streamlining, 5a choose the cut, 4 erosion, 6+7 waves and deposition, features, 8 soil/veg/wetness, 5b islands.
1. Uplift: low-frequency noise + tilt + edge taper. 2. Rock map: bands along the dip coordinate (random widths, wiggled), fault shifts, fault breccia lines; C takes rock from the lava-stack height (bole foot, vesicular top, columnar middle). Rock hardness also imprints on relief (hard stands up, soft hollows; glacial scour of soft rock smeared along the flow in A). 3. Streamlining by anisotropic noise (not a blur). Fine-scale relief is scaled by an envelope that is strong near the cut and subdued on high ground (gives many skerries with sane slopes; TUNING trick). 5a. The datum is a quantile: land fraction (A 13-20%, B 17-25%, C 12-18%, seeded) sets where 0 falls; below 0 a soft shelf break (`-D tanh(-h/D)`, D = 26/20/36 m). 4. D8 flow accumulation (sorted pass), stream power `dh = K * erodibility(rock) * sqrt(A) * S`, then thermal relaxation with a rock slope cap; 24-28 iterations. 6. Exposure = weighted open-water run over 16 bearings (stepped ray march, fetch capped at 40% of the world, weight peaking on the wave direction); wave erosion = band around sea level scaled by exposure, erodibility and a joint-spacing factor; 5 rounds. 7. Deposition: eroded matter plus a province shell-sand supply, drifted a few cells to the lee, laid where exposure is low and depth under 4 m, boosted between close islands (partial-height bars and tombolos, not full joins). Deposits become rock id `shell_sand`; storm-ridge `cobble_shingle` at exposed cliff feet (B, C). 8. Wetness (flow accumulation + flatness + lakes), loch depth by priority flood on land only, soil/vegetation by rock + slope + inland wind exposure + wetness (legend in `VEG_LEGEND`), `surface_colour()` for RGB.
Outputs on the `World` object: `height` (float32), `rock` (uint8 id), `exposure`, `wetness`, `veg`, `lake_depth`, `island_label`, `islands` (id, area, centroid, peak, max height, coast length, rock mix, size class, tide behaviour, landing candidates = gentle lee shore with depth offshore), `features` (stacks, arches with `needs_mesh_opening`, geos), `rocks` table (hardness, joint spacing, colour fresh/weathered/lichen, soil, vegetation, slope cap), `timings`. Arrays are `[row=y, col=x]`, row 0 south, x east, y north, z up.
Determinism: `world_bytes_hash` of height+rock+exposure is identical for the same (seed, province, size, n); tested. The terrain is defined in metres, so n=513 is the same world at finer cells (noise octaves finer than 6 m are dropped so nothing aliases).

## 4 Tuning, not fact
Every amplitude, wavelength, hardness ratio, slope cap, erosion constant, shelf depth, land fraction, shell-sand supply, tide amplitude and envelope rule is TUNING. Verified only as figures: Korcak 0.65, coast dimension 1.2-1.3, the Hebridean tilt, the machair mechanism, the stream-power form. The 512 m world compresses real archipelago scale by roughly 20-50x horizontally, so slopes read steeper than the research's real-scale ranges; do not read the slope rows as failures of geology.

## 5 Honest gaps
- Median land slope is 37 (A) and 48 (C) degrees, above the 5-25 research range; B is in band. Land below 10 m is too high for A/B (low relief). Korcak K is 0.45-0.54, below 0.65: too few small islands relative to large ones, worst for B and C. Share of worlds with K in band is 20% for B and C.
- Heightfield cannot hold overhangs: arches are a saddle just above high water plus a flag; notches, caves and column faces are for the mesh builder. Cliff notches in B are not in the field.
- Hand-rolled D8 does not route flow across land pits, so valleys do not breach lochs; no sediment transport model (deposition is a rule); rock is a 2-D map (strata are not 3-D, C derives rock from height).
- Island coast length uses a 0.785 staircase correction; islands under 4 cells are ignored; the largest island often dominates (largest share about 0.5).
- Province A colour reads dull grey-brown on the sheet (heather/lichen palette needs an art pass). Provinces P4-P8 are not implemented.
- Dry tidal bars exist only as partial-height deposits; no explicit sill/scour hole painting (sills fall out of saddles between peaks; scour holes are absent).

## 6 How it connects
- Seabed (`seabed.js`, `blender/island_common.py`): depth is now the field itself. `seabed.js` pieces that become unnecessary: the shelf curve `Dmax*prod(1-exp(-d/L))`, the distance-to-island transforms, the probabilistic union, channel-gap and sill construction, the sand-link (tombolo) rule (replaced by deposition + saddles), and the hand `BARS`. Still needed on top of the field: scour holes at narrow gaps (add after the field, from the gap width), the flow continuity rule (read depth and width from the field), kelp bands (rock id `hard bed` and depth 0.5-4.5 m on the game scale), landing windows (`dry_flat_width_m` and slope are already on each landing candidate). The page's tide level maps straight onto `land_mask`.
- Blender: `island_window(w, id, size_m=128 or None)` returns the island-centred sub-field (height, rock, exposure, wetness, veg, island mask, origin, cell size) and `island_camera(w, id, lens_mm)` returns position, target, lens, distance that frame the whole island (Blender axes = world axes). Rock id to material through the rock table (`colour_*`, `joint_m` for column or bedding scale, `soil`, `vegetation`).
- Page: `surface_colour(w)` is a ready RGB texture; `veg` and `lake_depth` drive planting and water; `out/world_*.npz` holds height/rock/exposure.
- Foundry: `checks.measure` returns the same kind of per-seed numbers as the foundry table (coves, landing candidates, shelf share) and could be a drop-in measure set. Wildlife/flow: `exposure` is the fetch input for wave and foam, lee low-exposure water for seals and drift collection, `rock` + depth for kelp, `wetness`/`lake_depth` for freshwater.
Run: `python3 landform/tests/test_world.py` (39 checks); `python3 landform/checks.py 20`; `python3 landform/sheet.py sheet|views|export`.

## 7 The Blender link (landform render v0)
`blender/landform_scene.py` draws islands from this tool instead of the invented island in `island_common.py`. Run (any Blender 3.6+ or the bpy module, CPU):
`blender -b --python blender/landform_scene.py -- --province A --seed 2001 [--view hero|wide|top] [--w 1200 --h 800 --samples 64] --out shot.png` (`--meta-only` builds no scene and prints the hashes; `--island N` forces an island). It prints one `LANDFORM_META {...}` line (mesh hash, island, landing, camp, camera, counts, timings).
How it works:
- `make_world(seed, prov, n=513)` (1 m cells), then `island_window` (island bounding box x1.5 + 44 m, max 300 m) or the whole 512 m world for the wide view. ONE terrain mesh (Catmull-Rom up-sampled 2x to 0.5 m for hero windows, about 360k vertices) holds land and seabed; the sea plane at z = 0 cuts it, so islands are peaks of one surface and the shelf shows through the water. The window rim sinks to deep water so the block has no wall. Rock ids are warped a little so contacts are wavy.
- Island choice (`choose_island`): largest island with bounding box <= 200 m, area >= 1500 m2, not touching the world edge, whose best landing scores >= 0.85. Landing (`pick_landing`) = the tool's candidates scored by kind (sand best), exposure, slope, length and dry room behind; if the tool has none (basalt) the island's shore cells are scanned with the same score. Camp (`layout_camp`): fire on the flattest dry ground 4-17 m behind the landing, two tents with doors to the fire, the rowboat above the high-tide line, a levelled 5-8.5 m pad (the only place the field is edited). Real metres: tent 3 m, boat 3.4 m.
- Colour is the tool's maps: vertex colour from `veg` (heather, pasture, turf, scrub, machair, rush, bog, loch), sand where `rock == shell_sand` or `veg == sand`, shingle, rock colours from the rock table (fresh on steep faces and the wave-washed splash zone, weathered and dulled on gentle ground, lichen from the table), bedding spacing from `joint_m`, bedding/foliation planes from the structure dip (`bed_normal`), vertical crack prisms for basalt. The shared rock shader (`li_scene.rock_nodes`) gained `palette`, `joint`, `bed_normal`, `columnar`, `wander`, `lichen`, `streaks` options (defaults reproduce the old look). The tide levels from the world darken the wet band between low and high water.
- Trees, scrub and ground cover: the species set of `blender/li_trees.py` (section 8). Boulders by exposure and steepness at the coast, big feature rocks on exposed shore, with the rock's own palette. Grass tufts by veg class (merged mesh through `make_tufts(sites=...)`, not true instances); heather is now the instanced purple-flecked patch of the species set (`make_tufts(..., heather=0)`).
- Sea: `li_scene.make_sea` with the depth image of the window (and wave exposure in G for a broader surf line on exposed shores), absorption plus a faint blue-green scatter so deep water is teal rather than black; sky and sun as the earlier hero (sun low, 125 degrees round from the camera). Camera: `island_camera` gives direction and lens, `fit_camera` then fits distance and centre to the projected island cells (the tool's own distance leaves the island small once foreshortened); it looks from the side the landing is on.
- Tests: `python tests/landform-render-test.py` with the bpy python (mesh hash, island and camp determinism, rule checks for all six worlds, tiny render not blank, shots under 1.5 MB, old batch 1122 hero + bake and the new `landform` batch phase).

### Honest gaps of the Blender link (v0)
- (fixed in v1, see section 9) Basalt (C) read worst: the rock map put 84% `bole_ash` (red clay) on the chosen basalt islands.
- Gneiss/sandstone shores show blocky white/pale squares along the coast (sand, tide band and splash zone follow the 1 m cells of the tool, only softened by a domain warp). Cliff faces are smooth meshes with shader bump; no overhangs, caves or stack openings (heightfield). Steep compressed relief makes islands look like hills with straight slopes.
- Grass tufts and heather are a merged mesh (about 2-4k tufts per hero), not true instances; they barely register at 7 px per metre. (Trees: see section 8; the single tiered conifer is gone from the landform scenes.)
- Lochs are dark glossy patches at ground level, not water surfaces. Boulders are lumpy ellipsoids with the rock shader; the large ones look like eggs.
- Camp is tiny at island framing (tent 3 m is about 20 px). Gneiss 2001 chose a scanned landing (score 0.90), not a tool candidate; basalt worlds have no tool candidates at all. Basalt 2201 hero is island 2 because island 1 (232 m) is over the size cap.
- Sea floor shows through shallows and shelf (teal), but steep coasts drop to dark water within a few metres. The sun/sky glow direction of the Nishita sky is not checked against the lamp. Mesh window beyond +-150 m is padded with the world's edge.

## 8 Tree variety pass (v1): `blender/li_trees.py`, `blender/tree_lineup.py`
One seeded procedural species set replaces the single tiered conifer in `landform_scene.py` (`li_scene.make_conifers` stays only for the invented legacy island). It is cool-temperate Atlantic: the real Hebrides are largely treeless, so woodland is sparse, sheltered and patchy, and most ground stays open (a design strength, not a flaw).
Species, each 3-8 mesh variants (numpy branching trunks, blob and leaf-card crowns, 100 to 2200 triangles each; wide view uses reduced-detail variants):
| group | species | where (maps) |
|---|---|---|
| upright trees | Scots-style pine (flat top, bare lower trunk, orange-grey bark, forked and veteran variants); spruce/fir (irregular drooping boughs, lean, dead-top, sparse and broad forms); birch (white bark with dark marks, airy card crown); rowan (round crown, berries on some); oak (broad, gnarled, best soil only) | pine: dry thin free-draining soil, any province, commoner on gneiss; spruce: deep soil, sheltered, gentle, B and C; birch: gullies and damp lee slopes everywhere; rowan: gully sides and crevices; oak: soil depth >= 0.3 m, wind < 0.28, gentle, B and C best |
| thickets | hazel (multi-stem), willow (sprawling, grey-green, narrow cards) | hazel: deep damp sheltered ground; willow: damp ground only |
| wind-shaped | wind pine (stunted, trunk leaning, crown streaming downwind), wind thorn (low wedge-shaped crown, sheared windward) | exposed band, wind exposure 0.28 to 0.78, soil >= 0.1 m; yawed and leaned away from the wave direction (`structure['wave_from']`) |
| scrub, cover | juniper, gorse (yellow flecks), bracken (autumn bronze fronds), heather (purple flecks), bog cotton | juniper thin soil; gorse coastal and slope; bracken deep sheltered slopes; heather on heather veg; bog cotton on bog and rush with wetness > 0.45 |
| dead wood | lichened snag, fallen log / driftwood | near the coast (snags on soil above the splash zone, logs also on sand and shingle) |
Rules: `species_weights()` in `li_trees.py` is a pure function of the maps (province, soil depth from the rock table thinned by slope and wind and deepened by wetness, wind exposure, slope, wetness, elevation, vegetation class, rock amount); nothing on rock (rock amount >= 0.35), slope >= 0.52, bog, rush, machair, sand, shingle, loch, bare or lichen rock, below 1.8 m (splash zone) or within 9 m of the camp fire. Clustering: low-frequency copse noise gives copse cores (dense, all ages), edge trees (small, sparse) and rare singles (skew to veteran); crowns may overlap a little inside copses. Age classes sapling 0.34-0.58 / mature 0.82-1.08 / veteran 1.12-1.32 of the variant height (veteran variants are gnarled with a dead limb; species without one use a mature variant); height also follows soil depth and wind exposure; lean = a few degrees random plus up to 9 degrees downwind; width and height jitter independently. Colour jitter per tree (hue, value, and an autumn amount that recolours patches of leaves, about 10% of broadleaves strongly) rides on the object colour and is read by the leaf shader through Object Info. Instancing: one Blender mesh per used (species, variant), linked duplicates (shared mesh data) per tree, so a hero is about 24k unique triangles of vegetation and 210-300k instanced; the wide view draws trees and shrubs 1.8x so they register at 3-8 px.
Test numbers (`tests/landform-render-test.py`): placement hash deterministic per (province, seed); unit checks of the weights (oak needs >= 0.3 m soil and shelter, upright trees zero above wind 0.45, pine refuses wet ground, willow needs damp ground, province changes the mix); no live tree on rock, steep ground, bog, sand, shingle, splash zone or the camp pad; vegetation under 400k instanced triangles in all six heroes and the wide view.
`blender/tree_lineup.py` + `lineup_labels.py` render `shots/trees-lineup.png` (three bands, every species, three age classes, same sun and sky; ground cover enlarged as captioned).
TUNING, all of it: province weights, thresholds, copse noise, ages and scales are mine. Honest gaps: crowns are blobs and cards, so at close range they read as stylised low-poly, not botanical; autumn colour is a coin toss per tree, not a season model; no understory shading between trees beyond a per-vertex shade; birch and willow cards are alpha-masked quads (a little slower to render).

## 9 Basalt rock map fix (v1, province C)
v0 labelled the flat tread of every lava flow `bole_ash` (red clay), so the chosen basalt islands were 84% red clay: red cones with no columns. Now (`world.rock_visible`, `C_RELIEF`): tread = `basalt_vesicular_top` (brown loam, grass), riser and every cliff face (slope > 0.8) = `basalt_columnar` (dark grey), a thin red bole band at the foot of each riser plus bole-soil patches on about 3.5% of the treads. The erosion passes still use the v0 labelling and relief constants, so the heightfield, the coast, the islands and their landing candidates are the v0 ones for the same seed; only the land above 1.5 m is compressed by C_RELIEF = 0.45 (the 512 m world squeezes real relief into a few tens of metres, which made 48 degree median slopes), so the shoreline contour does not move. Flat tops (slope < 0.3, wind < 0.78) are pasture, so plateaus are grass. Columnar basalt colours are darker and greyer (fresh 70,72,78; weathered 104,102,104; lichen 126,126,104). In Blender (province C only): crack lines 2.8x wider, cracks no longer fade in patches, lichen 0.85 instead of 1.7, streaks 0.9, shader crack cells 1.5 m, plus a hexagon-like bulge pattern (period 1.7 m, 0.55 m amplitude) in the terrain mesh on columnar faces so the prisms catch the low sun; red bole shows as a soil tint on the grass.
Provinces A and B: the world hashes of seeds 1122, 2001, 2101, 2201 at n=257 are identical to the previous commit (checked); the only shared-code changes are the optional `crack_w` argument of `rock_nodes` (default is the old value) and the C-only branches.
