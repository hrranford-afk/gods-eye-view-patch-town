# World view: the live page draws a different island group per seed (task 9, branch citylab/cloud-islands)

`islands_look.html?world=NNN` draws one exported world of the land formation tool (`landform/world.py`) instead of the six hand-placed islands. Twelve worlds are exported; the picker is `lab_index.html` and an on-page selector (top bar, only with the switch). Without `?world` nothing changes: default hash `8d962c0c`, 88,790 triangles, no `__li.world` (tested, and `look`, `landing`, `sea`, `flow`, `seabed`, `weather`, `boats`, `macro-wire`, `macro-sea` suites still pass).
Confidence tags: VERIFIED = measured by a test in this commit; PLAUSIBLE = reasoned, not measured; TUNING = my choice; UNVERIFIED = not checked.

## What was built
- `landform/export_world.py` (numpy only). Per seed: a 256 m window (D: 258 m) of the tool's n=257 field (2 m cells), centred on the start island, as `landform/worlds/seedNNNN/world.bin` (height int16 1/256 m, rock id, vegetation class, shore class, exposure, wetness, bed hardness; 133 kB), `manifest.json` (island table, start landing and normal, tool landing candidates, shore segments from `ecosystem/shore_catch.py`, rock colours, hashes, label) and `world.js` (the same bytes as base64 in a classic script, so the page loads from `file://` and from a server). `worlds/index.json|js` lists them. `python3 landform/export_world.py check` re-derives all 12 and compares the sha (VERIFIED, bit for bit).
- The 12 worlds: gneiss 153, 147, 62; sandstone 139, 23, 174; basalt 56, 99, 106 (the first three of each province in `foundry/seeds/shortlist.json` that pass the start rules on the current `world.py`; start island, landing and rules from `foundry/lfmetrics.start_island`); volcanic 4, 2, 5 (no foundry entry; seed 1 was replaced by seed 4 in the worlds fix, see 'The fix' below; the window is swept round the gentle landings for a real cone slope). Total 3.4 MB for the folder including the base64 copies (VERIFIED, test limit 6 MB).
- `world_view.js` (loaded only with the switch): bicubic height `H(x,z)` (the last 16 m before the table edge sink to deep water so no land is cut by the wall), terrain colour from the maps (veg class, shore class, the tool's rock colours, page tide zoning, noise, dirt round pads), content placement, and the seabed adapter.
- `islands_look.html` edits, all behind `WORLD`: table radius 122 and height texture 256 m (`TS=128`), sea shaders patched for the new `TS` and rim, `baseH` reads the world, `placeWorld` calls `WV.place`, terrain mesh at 2x the cell size, camera home and limits scaled, flow field cut-off scaled (`max(3600, (3.6a)^2)`, identical for the default islands) and flotsam spawn radius scaled, island list for flow, gulls and weather made from the world's largest islands, seed selector and `__li.world`.
- Content from the maps: trees by vegetation class, province, exposure, slope and a copse noise (`LI_KIT` conifer, broadleaf, shrub, tuft, rock, the same builders as the default page); rocks on the shore band and steep ledges; jetty at the start landing, out to water deeper than 1.9 m; 10 houses on gentle ground within 62 m, turned to the water; camp (fire, two tents, three people, beached rowboat) on a second shore of the start island; two moored boats pushed seaward until they float at the lowest tide; one sailing boat on a 12 m circle of open water; driftwood on beaches. Sea is `sea=2`; weather, macro sea, flow and boats=2 work on top.
- `lab_index.html`: the Lab index of this branch with the world picker (12 links with rock and island type). No index existed in the tree, so this is a new page.
- Tests: `tests/world-view-test.js` (below), `tests/world-view-render.js` and `tests/world-view-sheet.py`.

## What is still faked
- The tool's world is 512 m; the page shows a 256 m window, so islands at the edge are cut and fade into deep sea over the last 16 m. Real-metre scale: the tool compresses real archipelago scale about 20 to 50x, so slopes are steep (gneiss median 37 degrees) and read as hills; relief is not relaxed.
- Terrain is the 2 m field bicubic-upsampled; colours are 2 m class maps blended and noise broken. Shore foam and wet band come from the page shader on the height, not from the tool's exposure beyond the foam weight.
- Lochs are coloured patches on the terrain, not water surfaces. Rock in the tool is a 2-D map: no cliff overhangs, caves or stack openings, basalt columns are colour only.
- Village, jetty and camp are placed by rules from the landing sites, not designed; the start landing of most worlds is a "scan" site (the tool had no sand or ledge candidate).
- The seabed pass uses an adapter (`WV.seabed`): depth, bed hardness and exposure come from the field; stream speed uses width = twice the distance to land. Channel scour holes, sills and sand links of `seabed.js` are not built and its debug overlay is off in world mode. The flow field is the same analytic potential flow around the largest islands (as discs), not a solve on the real coast.
- Volcanic windows (4, 2, 5) show a 40 to 48 m slice of a cone flank (the tool's cone is 250 to 270 m high on a 600 m radius): a window of 258 m cannot hold the whole cone with its crater and the coast. They read as a steep dark flank next to a green ash apron and a black lava delta, not as a whole volcano island (see the gaps under 'The fix').
- Shadows: the shadow map stores heights up to 14 m, so peaks above that cast partial shadows.
- Sun glitter on the sea is large near the sun direction (the page's own sea shader at a larger table); pictures use a sun that keeps it out of frame.

## How HW orders can export more worlds
1. Add `(province, seed)` to `WORLDS` in `landform/export_world.py` (A, B, C need a start island that passes `foundry/lfmetrics.start_island`; run `foundry/sweep.py` and `shortlist.py` to rank new seeds).
2. `python3 landform/export_world.py` (35 s for 12 worlds, numpy only). It rewrites `worlds/seedNNNN/*`, `index.json` and `index.js`; commit them. Each world is about 133 kB (186 kB with its base64 copy).
3. Open `islands_look.html?world=NNN`. The picker and selector read the index, no page change needed. Run `tests/world-view-test.js`; change the "12" count in it.
A worker order for a new province needs only a rock table entry in `world_view.js` palettes (veg classes 0-19 are handled) and a tree weight row.

## The fix: volcanic worlds and gneiss worlds (task 'worlds fix', same branch)
Confidence tags as above. Before: the volcanic windows (1, 2, 5) were a flat lowland (land 2.5 m at the 98th percentile, an olive-lime ramp, 1 percent dark ground) and the gneiss worlds (153, 147, 62) were a grey-mauve smear (6.6 to 13.4 percent of the ground in the mauve hues). Pictures: `shots/worlds-fix-sheet.png` (before and after of 153 and 5, all 12 small and labelled), `shots/worlds-fix-1..2.png` (1200 x 800: the volcano coast, the gneiss coast). Made by `tests/world-fix-render.js` and `tests/world-fix-sheet.py`; frozen clock `seat=3`, sun 40/-65 (picture 2 uses sun 58/-110, the low orange sun makes lit gneiss read cream).

What changed:
- Exporter (`landform/export_world.py`): the province D window is no longer put 40 m inland of one landing. `pick_d_window` sweeps window centres (3-cell steps, 45 cells each way) round every gentle shore candidate and keeps the window with 30 to 70 percent land inside the page disc (radius 90 m), the candidate inside, and the best score of (98th percentile of the land height, capped at 45 m) minus a penalty for tall ground at the rim minus a penalty for being far from half land. TUNING. Result: land relief 98th percentile 41 to 43 m, top 44 to 48 m (VERIFIED, test). **Seed 1 was replaced by seed 4**: in seed 1 the cone is 500 m or more from any coast (the best window anywhere in its field has 25 m of relief and a 16 m 98th percentile), so no 258 m window can show a slope and a shore together. Seed 4 was chosen from a sweep of D seeds 3 to 16 (best window with a gentle landing and 30 to 65 percent land: seeds 4, 7, 8 and 10 gave 68 to 86 m of relief at the 98th percentile, seeds 3, 12, 14 and 15 under 16 m); it shows a cone flank with a crater rim beside a coast. The old `landform/worlds/seed0001/` folder is left on disk, unlisted in the index (nothing was deleted); the exporter no longer writes it.
- Exporter colours: `PALETTE_FIX` rewrites the manifest `rock_rgb` of the pink gneiss (grey-rust instead of mauve), the grey gneiss, schist, breccia and, for D, basalt fresh and weathered, scoria, ash soil, lava flow and pumice (dark basalt, brown weathered basalt, red scoria, dark ash). The tool's own table in `world.py` is untouched (its world hashes are unchanged); only what the page reads changed. The world.bin of A, B and C worlds is byte identical (the sha did not change); their manifests and `world.js` changed because the palette table is part of them.
- Page, province D (`world_view.js` `colourD`): ground colour from altitude, slope, the class maps and noise instead of the vegetation class ramp. Dark and pale ash on the gentle ground, pale grey-brown scree where the slope is 15 to 30 degrees, dark basalt faces on the steep, with downslope streaks (noise stretched along the fall line, so the barrancos and rain rills show), broad darker lava sheets and paler ash falls about 20 m across, red scoria only on the higher cones, black sand on the beaches, hardy olive scrub and thin grass only below about 7 to 24 m and only on gentle soil, patchy (an olive-lime lawn is gone). Trees: a third to a half of the old weights below 9 m, none above 24 m; shrubs fade with altitude. Boulders are dark. The edge of the table: tall ground rolls down to the rim over 44 m (16 m for the other provinces, unchanged) so a cone flank is not a vertical wall.
- Page, province A (`colourGneissRock`, `cellColour`): gneiss rock is banded: a foliation phase from height, a plan direction and noise gives light grey and dark grey bands, wide rust and tan staining zones, orange and pale green lichen patches on gentle weathered faces; steep ground turns to rock at a lower slope than before; heather is brown-olive with maroon patches and green turf where the wetness map is high or noise says sheltered (no more magenta wash); fewer pink heather flowers (30 percent of the heather shrubs, was 60).
- New helper for tests: `LI_WORLD.last.stats(n)` (the page's own colour function sampled on the land above 0.3 m: mean sRGB, hue, saturation, dark share, green share, mauve share, relief). Not part of the default page.
- Life layer: `landform/export_life.py` was re-run (the volcanic windows moved, so seal, kelp, shoal and robot sites moved with them); the A, B and C life files are byte identical.

Honest gaps, ranked:
1. The volcanic worlds are a 42 m slice of a 250 m cone, not a whole volcano island: no summit, and a crater only in some windows (seed 4 shows a rim and a bowl). A whole cone needs a scaled window (the island is 1200 m across, the page disc 244 m): the field would be resampled by about 1 to 6, which also means the life exporter (`export_life.py`) and every metre figure in it must be rebuilt on a scaled world. Not done.
2. Seed 1 is gone from the index (replaced by seed 4); anything that links `?world=1` still loads the old flat window from the stale folder, but it is not in the picker, the tests or the life sheets.
3. The cone flank is smooth at 3 m cells and the page mesh is 1 m: no individual lava blocks, no gully walls narrower than 6 m. Colour streaks fake the barrancos on the flat of the mesh.
4. Gneiss banding is a colour pattern on the 1 m vertex mesh (1.6 m on low quality): bands narrower than about 2.5 m alias; there is no relief in the rock faces.
5. Mean gneiss hue is olive-ochre rather than rust: the lichen and turf add yellow and green. Steep faces lit by the low orange sun read cream and peach in some shots.
6. Volcanic apron: black delta and beaches read well, but the green apron is still one soft green sheet at the home view; no ash-and-grass mosaic at that distance.
7. Boulders still use the page's generic rock mesh; the pink flower dots of heather shrubs are the page's, only fewer.
8. `assets/collector/world_robots/` still holds the GLB of the recipe that only retired world 1 used (nothing was deleted); `build_world_robots.py` now reads only the 12 listed worlds so the index and the test count 9 recipes.
9. The seal haul-out counts on the volcanic worlds (up to 132 animals on one site in world 4) come from the exporter's rule on a long black-sand flat, and were not tuned.
