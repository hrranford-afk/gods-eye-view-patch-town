# ROBOT_RADICAL: six new body plans for the collector family (23 Nov 2026)

The owner loves the robot recipes (`ROBOT_RECIPES.md`) and asked for radical new designs. This is the optional `plan` of a robot recipe: `robot_recipe_for(seed, radical=True)`. Off by default: the committed tortoise-plan family, its seeds 1 to 120 and the committed v2 GLB are untouched (the tests prove it). Rails as in STORY_BRIEF and COLLECTOR_QUIRKS_BRIEF: hopeful, nothing gory, no brands, no legible writing, the machines are fixed routines with no speech, no feelings and no eyes that look alive (the family face is still the dark visor band, the twin or trio windows, the slit or the tall window, never a round eye). The town's robot habit cards (tortoise ripples, terrier ears and tail light, wheelbarrow, beetle) were read for mood only; nothing is copied from them. No plan resembles a film robot: no ball with a head riding on it, no box with binocular eyes, no trash-compactor body.

## The seven plans
| plan | role | what it is | what makes it read at small size |
|---|---|---|---|
| `scout` | scout | the favourite look, forced: small (scale 0.78 to 0.90), three wheels a side (six wheels), floppy ears one and a half times as big as before (one up, one folded), mustard_clay paint about 6 times in 10 | a low yellow wedge on six fat wheels with one tall ear |
| `walker` | maintainer | 4 or 6 jointed legs (hip ball, thigh with an accent band, knee ball, steel shin with a rubber boot and a rounded foot pad), a rounded tub under the solar shell, a bin behind, the head pod in front. Six legs: a long low bug (tripod gait); four legs: a taller animal (trot). One leg is often short with a taped splint, feet and knees are a little off-true | legs splayed wider than the body, a knee bump every leg |
| `pontoon` | cleaner | twin torpedo floats (round or pointed bow) on three cross struts, a flat deck with non-slip strips, a rail, the head on a pedestal at the bow, the shell and a bin on the deck; a paddle wheel outside each float (open spokes, an eight-sided rim, blades) or a three-bladed prop behind each float | the two floats and the paddle wheel; a flat top |
| `spotter` | scout | tall and thin: a tiny body on 3 or 4 stilts (a tripod or a table), a big lookout pod with the lamp on top; or a telescoping three-section mast on a small six-wheeled base with guy lines, the pod at the top. Ears 55 percent of the time | height: 1.0 to 1.4 m against 0.6 for the tortoise, with almost no mass |
| `roller` | planter | a big ball-like wheel (radius 0.31 to 0.39, 0.46 to 0.60 wide): a painted ball with two rubber tread bands, or a bare rubber ball covered in lugs; a hub with an accent dish; two fork arms to a small cab with the shell on its roof, the head peering over the wheel on a long periscope neck, a tail boom with a caster wheel and the bin riding on it; half of them carry the seedling bed and a grow light | one huge disc and a thin neck |
| `snail` | hauler | a long low body on ONE wide belly track (real tread links in the hero), a low carapace of two solar halves, the head raised on a bellows neck, and the bin is a coiled drum standing up at the back with a spiral ridge on both faces (or nested rings and spokes), a hatch lid on top | the big round shell on a low slab |
| `crab` | sorter | a wide low body (0.62 to 0.74 m across) under a wide two-halved shell, a wide head on two stalks, two or three legs a side lying across and lifted at the knee, two big sorting claws (round palm, a fixed curved finger and a jaw that is its own node), held low, spread wide, or one raised with a bit in it; sorting trays at the front corners; side bins on the bin | a flat wide shape with two claws |

Mix rules (`robot_recipe.plan_for_seed`): seeds go in blocks of 16, a shuffled bag of 4 ear scouts and 2 of every new plan, so any 16 in a row hold 4 scouts (a quarter, the most common look) and 2 of each other plan. Roles: scout to scout, spotter to scout, walker to maintainer, pontoon to cleaner, roller to planter, snail to hauler, crab to sorter. Palettes are weighted per plan (scouts: mustard_clay 6 of 10; pontoons navy, cream and slate; snails teal, sage and plum; crabs brick and rose; and so on) and every one of the 11 palettes can occur. Lamp code, wear, quirks (0 to 3 of the eight slots, weighted by role, no lid quirk without a lid) and modules are drawn as in `robot_recipe.py`. The recipe keeps the committed fields (`role`, `paint`, `wear`, `lamp`, `quirks`, `modules`, `parts`, `body.shell`, `body.head`, `body.hopper`) and adds `plan`, `radical: true` and `body.dims` (the plan's numbers: leg count and gait, float length and drive, mast height, ball radius and style, coil radius and turns and style, claw pose and so on).

## How it is built (`blender/robot_radical.py`, python3 + numpy)
`collector_model.py` is not changed. `RadApplied` (a subclass of `robot_build.applied`) changes its module constants for the length of one build and restores them: paint, wear and lamp code of the recipe, the node and quirk lists, the bin dimensions of the plan (`bin_dims`), the extra clips. Each plan is a function that draws into nodes with the committed primitives, then the shared steps run:
- committed part functions used on every plan: `part_head` (visor style of the recipe) and `part_lamp` (moved to the plan's head position and scaled as a node), `part_shell_half` (two tip-up solar halves, with the shell dimensions and place of the plan, painted or solar, 1, 2 or 4 plates), `part_hopper` (the cargo bin at the plan's place; lids, modules, quirks and role parts attach to it as before), `part_tracks` (the snail's belly track), `part_wheels` (spotter mast base), the quirk functions (the tape quirk and the mirror move with the plan's body and head), the role parts (ears on the head, tool rack and side bins on the bin).
- new parts: `leg` (hip and knee balls, accent band, rubber boot, foot pad, optional taped splint), `plan_walker`, `plan_pontoon` (torpedo floats, deck, paddle wheel, prop), `plan_spotter` and `plan_mast`, `plan_roller` (ball, bands, lugs, hubs, fork, periscope, tail, caster), `plan_snail` (coil drum, spiral or ring face, hatch), `plan_crab` (claws, jaws, trays, stalks).
- node names: the 16 committed names exist on every plan (an unused one is an empty node, so the committed clips run on any plan; the arm nodes sit on the body top), plus the quirk, module and part nodes of the recipe, plus the plan's own: `leg_1` to `leg_6`, `paddle_l` and `paddle_r`, `wheel_big` and `caster`, `claw_l`, `claw_r`, `claw_l_jaw`, `claw_r_jaw`.
- new clips (added to the committed seven): `walk` (walker tripod gait, spotter slow stilt steps, crab sideways shuffle; loopable), `paddle_spin` (pontoon), `roll` (roller wheel and caster), `claw_sort` (crab jaws, in turn). They are rotations on the plan nodes.
- the scout goes through the ordinary `robot_build.py` builder with the look forced in the recipe (`robot_recipe._make_recipe` with `role_override` and `force`; the default call draws exactly as before) and the ears scaled up as a node scale.
- the root is a node scale (the page rotates nodes by name, so scale is free); the head scale is a node scale as before.

## What is faked
- Floats do not float: they are meshes on the sand, and the paddle wheels dip into nothing. The spotter's mast is three tubes and a collar, not an animated telescope; the guy lines are straight thin rods.
- Leg, claw and wheel clips are plain rotations of rigid nodes (a gait without inverse kinematics: feet slide a little and nothing checks collisions). They were built into the GLBs and listed, not played back in a viewer.
- Sizes and the overall scale are TUNING and node scales of drawn meshes (a scaled hero keeps its texel density). The scout's ears are the committed ears scaled 1.35 x 1.5 x 1.7.
- Wear is the committed baked shader with per-machine multipliers, so every plan shares the rusty-patch look; the shells share one solar-cell texture, the visor and lamp are the family ones.
- Plan-specific dimensions are chosen by hand per plan (`bin_dims`, shell place); the quirk positions of the snail are moved by hand (`qoff`) so that they sit on the drum.
- The preview camera is the committed three-quarter camera on sand moved out and up per plan (`preview_view`): tall machines are framed further out, so sizes in the sheet are not to scale between plans.

## Running it for an order (python3 + numpy for the models; Blender for the pictures)
```
python3 blender/robot_batch.py --radical --out OUT --phases recipes,models --jobs 4            # no Blender: 60 radical recipes and 120 GLBs (hero and live)
python3 blender/robot_batch.py --radical --blender BLENDER --out OUT --phases previews,variety,sheet --device GPU --gpu-name Radeon --require-gpu
python3 blender/robot_radical.py --seed 14 --out OUT --lods hero,live                          # one machine
```
`--radical` is off by default and changes nothing else: the same flags, `--seeds 1,5,9`, `--count N`, `--lods`, `--w --h --samples`, the picture guard, results.json with SHA-256 (it now records `radical`). Previews are framed per plan (`--zoom` overrides it for all). The robot-only variety number is skipped with `--radical` (every plan has its own framing, so the empty scene no longer matches). A radical sheet has the labels seed, plan, role, palette, quirks and parts. Writes only inside OUT, never deletes, never uses the network, the same seeds give the same recipes and GLB bytes. Timing: a hero is 35 to 60 s on one core, a live 5 to 8 s; a 640 x 420 preview at 24 samples is 10 to 25 s on four CPU cores.

## Honest gaps
- Same-plan machines are cousins: two snails or two pontoons differ in palette, shell face (spiral or rings), drive and details but share one body, so the judge still rates them 0.9 or more. The new plans differ strongly from each other and from the tortoise family, but within a plan the variety is smaller than across plans.
- The walker is the weakest design: sticks with knee balls and a box-like tub, charming mainly for the splinted leg and the stagger; the joints are balls, not designed joints. The crab's trays hang on thin rods and the raised-claw pose was reduced to the one raised claw holding a bit.
- Not checked in WebGL, on a phone, or with the new clips played: the walk, paddle, roll and claw clips are listed and built (the walker's six legs, paddles, wheel and jaws are real nodes) but nobody has watched them move; feet slide, claws can pass through the trays, and the committed arm clips run on empty nodes.
- The floats do not float: no water, no waterline, no wake. The mast does not telescope. The spotter's stilts and the roller's neck are thin and would need a stiffness check against a wind or a bump (a design question, not tested).
- Every plan shares the committed baked wear and solar texture, so they all rust in the same blotches; the scouts are the committed builder with the favourite look forced, not new geometry apart from the bigger ears.
- Quirk and module placements were looked at on 14 machines only; a quirk can collide with a plan part in a combination nobody rendered (for instance the extra shell on the crab's bin next to side bins). Sizes (bin dimensions, shell places, the roller's reach) are TUNING, and no plan comes from a real machine or from research: they are design.
- Heroes were not built for all 60 seeds (only the 14 of the sheet and the 8 of the tests), and no seed above 60 was built; the variety numbers cover one three-quarter view on flat sand with per-plan framing, so the robot-only measure is not available.
- The hardware orders are not written here (the owner packages them); nothing was run on any machine except this one.
