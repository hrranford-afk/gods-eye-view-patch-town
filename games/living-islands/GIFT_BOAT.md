# The Gift: a boat you can zip around in (Job 2)

A small tender you can row, and later drive with a found motor, round the home island and out to the skerry, with a wake, rocking, tide grounding, a small load, and seals and gulls that notice you. Behind the one gift switch. Software GL only was used to test it: there is no claim of speed on a real GPU, iPad or Radeon.

## Switches
- `islands_look.html?game=gift` is all you need. The boat arrives with the first order (the yard stage). `&boat=1` puts it at the jetty from the start (for a quick look). `&boat=0` turns the whole boat layer off (the page then behaves as the first day did, except for the kit hulls below).
- `&motor=1` starts a new boat with the small motor already fitted (try the fast boat without the first trip).
- `&errand=1` shows the errand stub in the Dock card (off by default).
- `&giftsave=1` allows save and load under automation (as in GIFT_FIRST_DAY.md); normal play saves by itself and the boat is part of that save.
- `&device=ipad|phone|desktop|radeon|ar` as in ULTRA_TIER.md. New: `&device=radeon` or `&device=desktop` opened on a built-in or mobile GPU becomes the ipad preset automatically, with a one-line note (see "The preset step-down"). `&stepdown=0` keeps the preset you asked for. `&gpuname=` and `&fakeua=` stand in for what the browser says, for tests.
- **BOLD-1** the gift now turns `boatkit=1` on (the baked dinghy, sailer and wreck instead of the old procedural hulls). The dinghy is the boat you drive, so the kit has to load. It also changes the sailing boat far out and the wreck on the camp beach to their kit versions. `&boatkit=0` brings the old hulls back (the drivable boat then falls back to the plain hull, still works).
- The default page (no switches) is untouched: hash 8d962c0c, 88,790 triangles (checked by the page test).

## How to play it
1. The first order is placed (day one, the yard stage). A line says a small rowing boat lies by the jetty, and a button appears bottom right: **Landing**. Tap the boat or the button: the landing card opens (what is aboard, the yard stock) with **Cast off**.
2. Driving. Touch: the round **stick** bottom left. Push it where you want to go on the screen: the boat turns to that heading and the push is the power; let go and it coasts. Pull straight back to brake and back water. Keys: **W A S D or the arrows** (ahead, port, astern, starboard), **space** stops, **E or Enter** is Dock. Mouse or finger: **tap the water** and the boat steers there by itself, round the island if it has to (a small ring marks the spot); any stick or key cancels it. The camera follows; drag, pinch and wheel still work.
3. The bay. You row at about 1.7 m/s (the page is a diorama: the table is 122 m across, so the outer skerry is about a minute away). The sea is not charted past the rim of the table; the boat stops there with a calm line. The tide matters: a boat grounds on the shallows at low tide (it slows and scrapes, nothing is damaged) and you cannot drive onto land; backing water always gets you off.
4. **First trip.** Row to the **outer skerry** (south-east, past the seals' rock). A crate is wedged on the high rock, and beside it a small motor in oilcloth. Tap **Dock** when the button says the skerry is near, then **Take the crate and the motor**. Bring it back to the **jetty** (or the beach landing), tap **Dock**, **Unload** (it goes into the yard stock, the stock button bottom left shows it), and **Fit the motor**: oars shipped, a motor on the stern, 8 units of load, 6.2 m/s, an exhaust puff and a bow spray at speed.
5. After that the skerry leaves one new crate a day (two or three units of planks, rope, glass or metal, by seed). Rowing out for it each day is a small loop; the motor makes it quick.
6. **Landings.** The beach landing, the jetty, and the skerry. Dock within about ten metres at walking speed: the boat slides to its berth by itself and the card opens. At the beach and jetty the card lets you **Unload** everything into the yard stock, **Load** two of a kind at a time from the yard stock, **Fit the motor**, and **Cast off**.

## What the boat does
- **Motion** (`boat_drive.js` part A, pure): a first-order speed with a time constant (1.2 s rowing, 0.95 s motor), turn rate that needs way on for the motor (the rudder) and less for oars (they can pivot), a slide and a heel away from the turn, a bow-up trim with the motor, a load that costs up to a quarter of the speed, a rough sea that costs a fifth, leeway from the wind (a gale pushes a rowing boat about 0.2 m/s). Rowing is pulsed by the stroke (40 percent drive, 60 percent glide, mean unchanged) and the stroke clock is the same one that swings the oars (`boat_motion.js` oarStroke, strokePeriod).
- **Bobbing and rocking:** three slow swells sampled at five points under the hull (centre, bow, stern, port, starboard), soft-capped like `boat_motion.floatMotion`. The sea plane itself is flat in the mesh, so this is cosmetic and does not follow the shader's waves exactly. A storm mood (the weather module's wind speed) multiplies the rocking about three times and takes a fifth off the speed.
- **Tide and land:** depth is tide minus ground height at five points under the hull. Under the draft (0.22 m rowing, 0.30 m motor) the boat is grounded: 25 to 100 percent of its speed, scrape foam; under 5 cm nothing moves it further in. A boat saved on dry sand can only move toward deeper water. The rim of the table is a wall.
- **The wake:** one mesh, one draw call, a pool of 104 quads: foam from both quarters and a patch behind the stern (thick and long with the motor, faint with oars), expanding ripple rings (stern with the motor, an oar ring at each catch when rowing), bow spray from 3.2 m/s (ballistic, 0.6 s), a grey exhaust puff only when the motor is on. Daylight tint at night.
- **The world notices:** the four seals (placeholder blobs, the gift has no animals layer) go ALERT (raise and turn toward the boat) at about 28 m for oars and 54 m for the motor when it is moving, and FLEE (slip into the water, come back after a calm spell) at 12 m and 30 m. The same two ideas as `animals.js`, with simple state. Gulls climb when a moving boat is under them (3 m for oars, 7 m for the motor, within 22 and 46 m).
- **No damage, nobody aboard, nothing gory.** Touching the shore only stops the boat and a line says so. No person is drawn; the load is little blocks (placeholder).

## The HUD
Bottom centre: a small compass rose (north is the world's minus z; the rose turns with the camera so the blue bow arrow shows where the boat points on the screen; the teal arrow is the wind, longer in a stronger wind), the speed in m/s with a word, and the load as units of capacity. Bottom right: one button, which reads **Take the boat** (afloat), **Dock** with the nearest landing and its distance, or **Landing** (at a berth). Bottom left: the stick. One calm line above the HUD says what is going on (aground, the first hint). While driving the gift hint bar and the camp chip are hidden. Touch targets are 52 px or more under the ipad preset (the page test measures them); at phone width (390 px) the pieces sit inside the screen and do not overlap.

## Save
The boat is a field of the existing gift save (`boat`): position, heading, motor fitted, a part in hand, load, trips, the day the skerry crate was last taken, landing docked at. A save while afloat comes back afloat in the same place (tap it to take it); docked comes back docked. Old saves with no boat load fine (no boat yet). `SAVE_VERSION` is unchanged; the boat is sanitised on load.

## The errand stub (`&errand=1`, off by default)
From a landing card: **Send it on an errand**. The boat rows a fixed path round the island to the skerry by itself (the path is found on the page's own depth, with a hull and a half of margin), waits six seconds, comes back to its berth, and the day's crate goes into the yard stock (a first-day motor part waits in the boat). It needs the day's crate to be there and room for it. No one is named, counted or shown; the boat is unmanned like the delivery tender, and the only record is a counter of errands in the save. It is a stub: fixed destination, one crate, no failure cases, no weather refusal.

## The preset step-down
The owner's iPad measurement (`research/RADEON_FIRST_MEASUREMENT.md`): `device=radeon` on an iPad ran at 28.6 fps with a tripled load. Now `LI_ULTRA.deviceStepDown` (pure, `ultra.js`) decides: `radeon` or `desktop` on a mobile device (iPad, iPhone, Android, or an iPad asking for the desktop site: Mac user agent with touch points) or on an integrated GPU (Intel, Apple, built-in AMD, Adreno, Mali) runs the **ipad** preset, and a one-line note shows for fourteen seconds at the top: "This looks like a mobile device, so the iPad preset is used instead of radeon. Add &stepdown=0 to keep radeon." A real graphics card, software rendering (the tests), and every other preset are left alone. An explicit `&q=` still wins over the preset's defaults. The decision is made before the renderer exists, from a throw-away context's GPU name.

## Honest limits
- Software GL only. No real GPU number. The pictures are software renders.
- The drive is tuned by feel in a software renderer that gives about one frame a second: the numbers (speeds, turn rates, radii) are guesses to be tried on a real device. The test advances the simulation in fixed steps, which is exact for the code and says nothing about how it feels at 60 fps.
- The island is small and the world ends at a rim 115 m from the centre: the motor boat crosses the bay in about twenty seconds. There is no second full island, only the two skerries; the trip goal uses the nearer one.
- The jetty is a short stub on a shelving beach: its berth is the nearest water that floats a rowing boat with the tide half a metre down, which is a few metres off its side, not alongside the deck. At the lowest tide (-1.1 m) the berths are dry and the boat sits grounded until the tide comes back.
- The boat has no sound (by design: the exhaust puff is visual only).
- The wind is a fixed direction with the weather module's speeds; the compass is honest only in that sense.
- The camp's own chip, the hint bar, and the gift's right-hand buttons are hidden or left alone while driving; a very small phone in landscape is not tested.

## Open flaw: the collector robot's head
The owner asked that the collector's dome with a blue grid read clearly as a solar canopy and not as a visor. The shell halves are the blue cells; the part that reads as eyes is the head pod: `COLLECTOR_MODEL.md` describes a wide dark visor band with a glow strip in the **baked atlas** of the `head` node. Changing it means editing `blender/collector_model.py` and re-baking the GLB with Blender, which is not installed in this sandbox (no downloads allowed), and a runtime recolour of a baked atlas would only move the problem. **Not changed. Open flaw, for the next Blender session.** FIXED rule 2 (robots are relics, no eyes that look alive) says this must be resolved.

## Open taste questions
1. Should the boat arrive with the first order (now), or only on day two, so the first day stays about the jobs?
2. The jetty berth sits off the side, not at the deck: build a longer jetty (a repair job) or leave it as the beach shelves?
3. Arcade or realistic handling: the stick gives you a heading and a power (arcade). The keys steer like a boat (realistic). Keep both?
4. One crate a day from the skerry is a small loop. Should the skerry crate grow with the years (or with the storms: Job 3) or stay small?
5. Seals flee quickly at a motor: is that the right trade (fast boat against wildlife), or should the hide build calm them?
