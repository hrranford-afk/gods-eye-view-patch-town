# The Gift · Opening film

## Camera plan · 90 seconds

**0–9 s, Night watch.** Skim offshore at swell height, letting the lighthouse sweep reveal a boat for one beat. **9–20 s, First light.** Match the white beam to a strip of gold on the water and climb until all four islands surround the central current. **20–36 s, Working quay.** Cut forward to evening and track alongside a crate on its way down the finished jetty. Pass the four stock bays and end at a taut mooring rope. **36–47 s, Long swell.** Follow the rope out towards the first rise of water. Let the swell briefly hide the far shore. **47–59 s, Great Wash.** Cut wide and hold the horizon steady. Follow one broken jetty section until a rope snags a crate and the whole tangle begins to turn together. **59–70 s, Wrack line.** Cut the roar. Creep sideways past the grounded hulk and still robots in grey dawn, with an occasional drip from the broken dock. **70–81 s, Arrival.** Move at rowing speed just above the bow. Let the snapped piles slide apart to reveal the landing. **81–90 s, First repair.** Cut close on the battery latch. Stop the camera when the gloved hand withdraws, hear the relay click, and hold on the light in the machine’s lens.

## Continuity and handoff

Use frame 02 for geography. The current sits in open water between the camp, split rock, lighthouse and lone tree. Alongside the eight frames, the jetty reference gives the shoreline sightline to the same centre. The turning debris gives the audience evidence of a pull beneath the water. Its origin can remain an open story question.

The jetty should be recognisable on both sides of the cut from storm to wreck. Carry one painted mooring post or one ochre float through shots 03, 05 and 06. A two-note winch sound in the working colony can return quietly on the robot’s first movement. For the transition to play, let the player take the final battery action on first viewing, or start them beside the freshly repaired machine.

## Likely production challenges

These are visual targets. Device costs need measuring in the game.

- **Rope and debris binding.** Author the snag and tension of two visible ropes. As their slack runs out, group the captured planks and crates into one moving raft. Let a few outer pieces remain loose. This preserves the moment the mass forms.
- **Breaking wave and central whirlpool.** Stage the large crest as an animated mesh with a timed foam pass. Give the central current a separate flow field shared by foam and debris. Keep the four island silhouettes readable behind the spray.
- **Light shafts and wet reflections.** Test a low-resolution lighthouse beam and a moving band of water highlights on iPad. Spend extra desktop resolution on reflections only after the silhouettes read at game distance.
- **Close repair interaction.** Set fixed poses for battery, socket and gripper. Time the latch sound and lens light to the same contact frame so a small movement carries the ending.
