# What Living Islands takes from City Lab (read, do not copy)
1. REUSE as is: `rtk.building` recipes and the validator (a tent, a bunk, a landing stage, a timber stack are data); the City Lab "What changed and why" log (one sentence per change, with before and after numbers); deterministic seeded simulation with plain-JSON state and a state hash; paired experiments (two choices, identical start, three identical runs); the safe-publish checker; the shared job board.
2. MEASURING (C03b): ingredients are charged on delivery, so the first minutes of any run include a one-off stocking cost. Measure after a warm-up and over whole delivery cycles. For the island: measure after the first unload has settled, and over whole tides.
3. A SHORT SHORTAGE CAN LOOK PROFITABLE (C03b): stock covers the gap while less is spent. Always show stock next to any "saving".
4. ACCOUNTING RULE TO DECIDE UP FRONT: City Lab pays wages only for filled places and charges ingredients on delivery. Living Islands has no wages in the first tide; it has labour and stock. Decide early what a person who cannot reach work costs (here: nothing is paid, but their task slips and is shown).
5. SHORTAGE RULE (C06): a short supply is shared in proportion to capped demand ("rule A"). The first-tide analogue: when hands are short, jobs slip in a stated priority order, visibly.
6. DESIGN FINDING: an upgrade pays only if the constraint it removes is the one that binds (a faster oven did not pay while customers were the limit). For the island: a landing stage pays because the tide window binds, not because carrying is slow.
7. PROCESS: paper test first, then a headless proof, then a page; every claim names a file and a command; the owner decides direction; no pictures of the owner's rooms or tables in the public repository.
