# PatchTown: how it gets to mega

Honest review, 16 Nov 2026. Written for a phone.

---

## 1. The verdict in 5 lines

1. **What is special:** the game knows your real room, remembers it for days, and turns your sofa into the level.
2. Nobody else ships this for families on an iPad. Niantic announced in April 2026 that it is shutting its home-pet game, Peridot.
3. Your privacy rule is a real selling point: no photos kept, nothing leaves the iPad.
4. **The biggest risk:** almost nothing has been played on a real iPad, in a real room, by anyone but you.
5. 29 builds pass robot tests in fake rooms. The first real room lock was today. Build one fun loop on the device before anything else.

---

## 2. What it's missing: the top 10 gaps

Ranked by how much each one decides "people love it and come back".

### Gap 1. A room start that works every time

- **Why:** the screen sticker locked today, but markerless relock failed in the shed. HelloXR also loses its place after sleep. If the room doesn't lock, nothing else matters.
- **Evidence:** Apple's own saved room maps (world maps) also fail when the light changes. So the sticker stays useful even in a native app.
- **Fix:** make the TV or printed sticker the normal start, not the backup. Call it the "home stone". Then build the native app once and add world maps on top.
- **Effort:** 2 days for the web. 1 to 2 weeks for the native app.
- **Who:** cloud session. You install Xcode and press Build (about 1 hour).

### Gap 2. One finished 10-minute loop, not six half-modes

- **Why:** the plan has 6 modes. A new player needs one that is great.
- **Evidence:** Thronefall sold 1 million copies in its first year. It is small, forgiving, and you build only on set spots. The first-10 robot replay scored only 5 out of 10.
- **Fix:** Sofa Keep v1. Build on 3 spots, then 3 waves, 1 turret, then dawn. It should take 10 minutes from start to the crown.
- **Effort:** 7 to 10 days.
- **Who:** cloud session.

### Gap 3. Real players, and reports that come home

- **Why:** robots can't tell you if it is fun. Only faces can.
- **Evidence:** every lock fault found so far appeared only on a real device.
- **Fix:** the iPad sends a short report to the Mini after each game. It holds counts only: lock time, waves won, minutes played, quits. It never sends who or where. Add a 3-question smiley form at the end.
- **Effort:** 3 days.
- **Who:** cloud session builds it. You run 5 test sessions with friends.

### Gap 4. Arms get tired, and the iPad gets hot

- **Why:** holding up an iPad for 20 minutes hurts. People quit without saying why.
- **Evidence:** in a study of an iPad table AR game, players reported tired arms and awkward poses. Many Pokémon Go players switch the camera AR off.
- **Fix:** add "Prop mode": lean the iPad on a stand and aim it at the sofa. Add a map view: a dolls'-house view of your scanned room with no camera. Waves should pause when the iPad is put down.
- **Effort:** 4 days.
- **Who:** cloud session.

### Gap 5. Playing together on the sofa

- **Why:** the best home games are played with someone next to you.
- **Evidence:** Minecraft Earth closed because it needed people to gather, and Covid stopped that. Pokémon Go's friend features made 113 million friend links. Orcs Must Die! Deathtrap was balanced for 4 players.
- **Fix:** player 2 uses a controller on the Mac, shown on the TV. The same TV shows the sticker, so both players share the room. Player 2 takes a second turret, or plays the attackers.
- **Effort:** 5 to 7 days.
- **Who:** cloud session.
- **Your call (money and security):** 2 iPads in one game needs an online service.

### Gap 6. A reason to come back tomorrow that isn't a chore

- **Why:** timers and paywalls drive people away.
- **Evidence:** Peridot made people pay about $5 to hatch a new pet. It earned only $2.9 million and closed. Wizards Unite closed after installs fell 57% in a year.
- **Fix:** "The night siege". It runs while you sleep, using the away-time engine you already have. In the morning the real sofa shows what happened: a broken gate, a stuck ladder, a gift. Nothing is lost for good. The weekly mega project is the long goal.
- **Effort:** 4 days.
- **Who:** cloud session.

### Gap 7. Easy to read at arm's length, and punchy to play

- **Why:** small toys on a busy real carpet are hard to see.
- **Evidence:** the Bad North makers said their core value is "readability". They said sound helps you read the fight.
- **Fix:** give each team one bold colour. Show enemy outlines through furniture using depth. Give each enemy type its own sound before it appears. Hits need a big squash, a pause and a "bonk".
- **Effort:** 4 days.
- **Who:** cloud session, plus sound and picture ideas from the town.

### Gap 8. Moving real things has to matter in the fight

- **Why:** this is the one thing only your engine can do.
- **Evidence:** Meta's First Encounters is the mixed reality game reviewers loved most. Its big moment is creatures breaking through your real walls.
- **Fix:** let a real mug or book block the attackers' path. The land grid already knows clutter. Attackers walk around it, so you plan the defence with your hands.
- **Effort:** 5 days.
- **Who:** cloud session.

### Gap 9. One look, picked by you

- **Why:** mixed styles look cheap.
- **Evidence:** the town's picture maker failed the style test 3 times. It could not draw a fort on a sofa.
- **Fix:** keep the toon style and free props you already have. Pick 1 of 3 style shots, with your eye as the judge. Don't wait for AI art.
- **Effort:** 2 days.
- **Who:** you pick. The cloud session builds it.

### Gap 10. How other people get the game

- **Why:** HelloXR is an odd extra step for a family, and iPad Safari has no AR.
- **Fix:** the native app goes out through TestFlight to about 20 testers.
- **Effort:** 2 days after the native build.
- **Who:** cloud session. **Your call (money):** this needs Apple's developer membership, a yearly fee.

## 3. Three bold bets

| Bet | Pitch in one line |
|---|---|
| A. Night Siege | Your sofa gets attacked while you sleep. Check the damage over breakfast. |
| B. TV Portal | The TV locks the room AND is the second player's window. |
| C. Lights Low | A 10-minute grown-up horror night in your own lounge. |

### Bet A: Night Siege

- **Why it could work:** the moment people talk about is a morning surprise in their own room. You already have away time and the diary.
- **1-week test:** you plus 2 friends' homes play for 5 mornings. Count how many mornings each one opens it without being reminded.
- **Proves it:** 3 or more mornings out of 5. **Kills it:** 1 or fewer.

### Bet B: TV Portal

- **Why it could work:** today's only real-device win was the screen sticker. Turn that win into the feature. Couch co-op needs no online service and no second iPad.
- **1-week test:** Mac on the TV plus iPad, 10 sessions. Count the lock time and whether both people want "one more go".
- **Proves it:** locks in under 5 seconds 9 times out of 10, and 7 of the 10 sessions end with "again".

### Bet C: Lights Low (adult horror)

- **Why it could work:** horror is one of the most-played kinds of mixed reality. Quest has Panic Room, Shattered and Hauntify. Phone AR horror (Night Terrors) did most of its scaring with sound. Your engine knows where the sofa edge is. It knows what is behind the sofa, and which way you are looking.
- **1-week test:** one 10-minute night with 1 creature type and no story. Test it with 3 adults. Ask: "Did you check behind the sofa afterwards?"
- **Proves it:** 2 out of 3 say yes.
- **Your call:** the separate link and how dark it goes.

## 4. Training our own models: the blunt version

### What your hardware really does

| Machine | Training truth |
|---|---|
| Radeon VII on the Intel Mac | No CUDA (Nvidia's training system). No MLX, which is Apple-chip only. PyTorch stopped Intel Mac builds at 2.2 in Jan 2024. Apple's current page lists Apple chips only for GPU training. |
| Same card | 4-bit training tools (QLoRA, Unsloth) are built for Nvidia, so they are out. |
| Dell and Mill | Processor only. Fine for tiny models. Hopeless for language models. |

The town already trains tiny experts in 25 to 55 minutes. Their own notes say they are bad at "questions, lists, JSON, sense". In the 15 Nov results, about 1 in 8 answers came back empty or broken.

### Not worth it

- **Training your own big LLM.** No. It takes thousands of top GPUs.
- **A live LLM inside the web game.** It means a big download, a hot iPad and slow replies. It also can't be checked before kids see it.
- **"Scary" from a model.** Fear comes from timing, silence and where things are. A random text model breaks the timing.
- **Model-made 3D art.** It needs a download, and the picture test already failed.

### Worth it, in this order

1. **The Wave Director. It is not a neural net, and it is the best value.**
   - **What:** a settings table of about 20 knobs. They set wave size, timing, routes, and when help arrives.
   - **How:** the Mini plays thousands of robot sieges a night in the test world. A simple search keeps the settings that give "close calls".
   - **Data:** robot games only, so nothing personal.
   - **Time:** 1 night per round.
   - **Success:** the base ends a wave with 1 to 2 hearts left in 40 to 60% of waves. Nobody loses all 3 waves in a row.
2. **The Bark Book.** These are short creature lines, written ahead of time and checked before they ship.
   - **Step 1, prompt only:** the Brain writes 2,000 lines over 1 night from 20 examples. You thumb up or down 200 of them on your phone (about 20 minutes).
   - **Step 2, only if fewer than 3 in 10 are kept:** fine-tune a 0.5 to 1.5 billion parameter model on about 500 good lines. Use the town's own trainer if it can do LoRA, a cheap add-on training method. Expect about 1 night.
   - **Success:** blind test, with you keeping 6 or more lines in 10.
   - Lines ship as plain text. No model runs in the game.
3. **Tiny surface classifier.**
   - **What:** it learns "sofa edge or not" and "climbable or not".
   - **Data:** the engine's own numbers (height, size, flatness). No photos.
   - **Size:** a few thousand numbers. It trains on the Mill in minutes and runs on the iPad.
   - **Success:** it beats the current rules on 50 labelled test-world rooms.
4. **Creature voices.**
   - **What:** a small open voice model (Kokoro, 82 million parameters, Apache licence) pre-records lines in the town. Then they are warped into creature voices in the game.
   - **Your call (download):** about 300 MB. One part it needs (espeak-ng) has a GPL licence, which can force you to share your own code. Check the licence before you ship.

### A safer route worth knowing

The native app can use Apple's built-in on-device model, about 3 billion parameters. It runs on iPads with an M1 chip or newer. It is free, works offline and sends nothing out. It could make creatures react to "the speaker moved", using labels only. Keep it for family-safe, checked phrasing.

## 5. The adult horror version

### What makes it truly scary in AR

- **It is your own room.** The fear stays after the game ends, so leave small traces. Next day, a scratch mark shows on the real shelf where it hid.
- **It moves only when you are not looking.** The engine knows where the iPad points. Turn back and the thing is closer. You can't do this in a film.
- **Behind things.** Depth lets it hide behind the real sofa. Show a hand on the edge first and the body later.
- **Sound before sight.** With headphones, a scratch comes from behind the sofa. Then silence, then something small. Use few big scares.
- **Climbing towards you.** Small things crawl up the sofa front towards where you sit. The sofa keep becomes a trap.
- **Rest beats.** Tension, then relief, then tension. Too many scares and people get used to them.
- **Our own creatures:** for example, a pale crawler made of dust and hair. Nothing from films or games.

### Guard rails

| Rule | How |
|---|---|
| Age gate | A separate link and app section. Ask for the age at start. **Your call:** 16+ or 18+. |
| Phobia mode | No legs (blobs instead of spiders), no faces, no teeth. |
| No jump scares | A switch. Tension stays, sudden loud hits go. |
| Instant stop | Put the iPad face down: it pauses, the screen brightens, creatures leave. |
| Safe body | Play sitting down. Never make people back up or walk in the dark. Keep the lights low, not off, because tracking needs some light. |
| Flashes | No fast flashing (seizure safety). Cap the loudness. |
| Clean ending | Every night ends with the creature visibly leaving. Then a lights-on screen. |
| Family wall | A separate save. Base Camp never shows horror. Family games keep "nobody dies". |
| Privacy | It never claims to "see" real people. No names, no photos, nothing into any model. |

## 6. The 4-week plan

The cloud session builds in the day. The town runs robot games, the Brain and pictures at night.

### Week 1: make it work on the real iPad

- Cloud: home-stone start, field reports to the Mini, Prop mode.
- Cloud: get the native app ready to build. You install Xcode and press Build (about 1 hour).
- Town at night: test-world robot runs. 3 style shots, built from shapes first.
- You: play 3 times for 10 minutes. Pick a style.
- **Done when:** it locks in under 5 seconds, 9 times out of 10, on your iPad.

### Week 2: one great mode

- Cloud: Sofa Keep v1 (3 spots, 3 waves, turret, dawn), then readability and sound.
- Town at night: Wave Director rounds and 2,000 barks.
- You: 2 test sessions with friends. Thumb the barks.
- **Done when:** a new player wins their first crown in 10 minutes without help.

### Week 3: together and tomorrow

- Cloud: TV Portal co-op (Bet B), Night Siege (Bet A), real-object barricades.
- Town at night: tune the Wave Director with 2 players.
- You: run the Bet A morning test with 2 friends' homes.
- **Done when:** 3 or more mornings out of 5, and 7 of 10 co-op sessions end with "again".

### Week 4: listen, then choose

- Cloud: fix the top 5 complaints from the field reports.
- Cloud: if bets A and B pass, build the 10-minute Lights Low test on a gated link.
- Town at night: Bark Book step 2, only if needed.
- **Your calls:** Apple's developer fee for TestFlight, the horror age line, 2-iPad online play.

**Don't start yet:** Raid, Hold the table, Squad tactics. Bring them across once Sofa Keep keeps people coming back.
