# Council round 4: Base Camp, making things (set 12 Nov 2026, from the game session)

Harry's question, in his words: "If you pick certain parts to map… do different rooms have different characteristics where they
can get different things or do stuff, and then they get to make things and use the conveyor belts? … a choice between an auto scan
… and an aimer, so you can actually designate parts?"

Same rules as before: each resident answers alone and sees no one else's ideas first. Words are saved untouched. Every idea keeps
"by". Count honestly. Keep answers short; small models do best under 120 words a question.

## Context for every resident (give this first)

Base Camp is an AR game on an iPad. Tiny creatures (Glip, Grizzle, Gloop, Glimmerfluff, Pip and friends) live on the real surfaces
of a real room: the floor, a desk, a shelf, a windowsill, a speaker top, a worktop. The iPad scans the room by itself. The creatures
build huts, farms, wells and lookouts, and carry things between surfaces in little cable cars. A tidy room gives them more land.
Already, some surfaces give a little more of one thing (a sunny windowsill grows food, a high shelf makes a good lookout).
Nobody dies in this game; it stays funny and kind.

## Questions

**M1. What each surface gives.** Pick five real surfaces or places in a home (any rooms: kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, shed, hall).
For each: what the creatures can gather or make there that they can't get anywhere else, and one funny problem with that place.

**M2. Making things.** Invent three things the creatures can make by combining what different surfaces give (A + B = C). Say what
each one is for. Keep the recipes short.

**M3. Moving things.** Cable cars already carry things between surfaces. Invent two more ways to move goods around a real room
(belts, chutes, slides, pulleys, anything). How does each one look, what goes wrong with it, and why is it fun to watch?

**M4. Choosing places.** The game scans by itself. Should the player also be able to point at a real spot and say "this one is
for…"? If yes: what can the player mark, and how does marking feel like play, not like a form? If no: why not?

**M5. Different rooms.** The creatures can live in more than one room of a house. What makes a kitchen colony different from a
bedroom colony or a shed colony? What do rooms trade with each other?

**M6. The first five minutes of making.** A new player has one colony on a desk. What is the very first thing they make, and what
small "wow" tells them there is much more to make later?

**M7. Too much?** These games can turn into busywork. Name one rule that keeps making things fun and calm, not a chore.

**M8. Water (Harry's idea, by Harry).** Crops need water, and a home has many sources: the toilet, the sinks, the bath, a fish
tank, and more. Each source gives a different amount and quality of water: some is great for plants, some is bad for the creatures.
Harry's rule: bad water is never deadly, but it is funny and it can hold back growth. For three water sources: what the water is
like, what it does to plants and to creatures (funny, never cruel, and how it slows things down), and how the creatures get it back
to the colony. Then: one way to make bad water good.

**M9. The ceiling (Harry's question, by Harry).** The iPad can now see the ceiling. Harry: "that doesn't look like it's
really designed to be up there… maybe points to run cable cars through, or whatever we haven't invented yet." For now the
ceiling holds only hooks that cable cars swoop through, lanterns and one sock lookout nest. Invent two more things that
belong ON a ceiling for thumb-sized creatures (not a normal camp turned upside down). What does each do, how do they get
it up there, and what funny thing goes wrong?
