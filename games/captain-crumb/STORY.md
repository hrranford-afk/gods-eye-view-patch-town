# Captain Crumb's Big Adventure: story outline (draft 1)

An AR story game for iPad. Captain Crumb, the brave little toast-coloured racer from Baguettes & Ballistics,
is lost in YOUR house. You walk him home through your real rooms, seen through the iPad camera.

Status: outline for Harry to approve. Nothing built yet. Names and lines are easy to change.

## The hook

Last night, after the Mealtime Mayhem war, Baron von Baguette snuck out of the bread bin and carried the Toy Box
away. Captain Crumb woke up alone on your kitchen worktop. The Toy Box (his home, and all his friends) is somewhere
in your house. He is two centimetres tall. Your house is enormous.

## How it plays

- **The world is your real house.** The iPad scans the surfaces around you (worktop, table, floor, sofa, shelves)
  and the game builds each chapter's path on top of them. Every player's adventure is in a different house.
- **You guide Crumb.** Tap where he should go, or draw his path with your finger (the My Track tool). He walks,
  climbs, slides and drives his little car where he can.
- **You help, he does it.** You can place a few helpful things: a ramp (a ruler), a bridge (a spoon), a zip line
  (string). That's the puzzle: get him across gaps and down from heights using the few things you have.
- **The Baron hunts him.** Sometimes you have to hide Crumb behind real objects in your room while the Baron's
  bread-crumb spies roll past.
- **Talk boxes.** The same 16-bit talking portraits as the toy game, with the same cast: Crumb, the Baron,
  Sticky Rick, Nana Nutmeg.

## Chapters (each 5 to 10 minutes)

1. **The Worktop (tutorial).** Crumb wakes up next to the kettle. You learn to guide him: walk, jump a gap,
   use a ruler as a ramp. Nana Nutmeg's voice crackles over a walkie-talkie: "The Baron took the Toy Box,
   dearie! Follow the crumbs!" A trail of crumbs leads to the edge of the worktop.
2. **The Long Way Down.** Getting from the worktop to the floor. A tea-towel abseil, a drop onto a cushion.
   First glimpse of the Baron's spies.
3. **The Floor Is Lava (well, Floor).** Crossing the kitchen floor in his car. Avoid the giant slippers.
   Sticky Rick shows up, jam-covered, as an unlikely ally, and offers a lift for a price.
4. **Base Camp Sofa.** The sofa is a mountain range. Climb the cushions, cross the armrest ridge, find a lost
   coin (treasure). Rest at a camp made from a bottle top. A quieter chapter: Crumb talks about home.
5. **The Shelf of Doom.** The Baron's lair is on a high shelf. Stealth chapter: hide behind real objects,
   time your moves when the spies look away.
6. **The Rescue.** Free the Toy Box. The Baron's last stand: a chase back across the room on a track you draw
   in real time. Ending: Crumb and friends home, the Baron sulking in the fruit bowl. Post-credits: the Baron
   plotting with a croissant.

## The look

- Toy-like and warm, the same family as Baguettes & Ballistics, but a bit more detailed because the camera
  is close.
- Crumb is always lit by a soft warm glow so he reads against any real room.
- Game things (paths, crumbs, ramps) have a hand-made craft look: paper, string, sticky tape, felt-tip arrows,
  as if a child built the adventure in their house.
- Calm music while exploring, sneaky music for stealth, big toy-orchestra music for the chase.

## Why it is good

- Your own house becomes an adventure world. Nobody else's game looks like yours.
- It reuses what we have built: AR, surface scanning, drawing paths, the cast and their talk boxes.
- Short chapters suit the iPad and AR (arms get tired).

## Test chapter (what we build first)

Chapter 1, The Worktop, about 5 minutes: scan the worktop, place Crumb, guide him to 3 spots, one ruler ramp,
one jump, Nana on the walkie-talkie, end at the worktop edge. If it feels magical on Harry's worktop, build the rest.
